# OSD 0.7 — specification

> **Candidate build, not published.** OSD 0.7 is a candidate, not accepted: it may change before its acceptance. This text is a copy of `spec/0.6.md`: 0.7 opens with its change of address alone (the `$schema` of a descriptor names 0.7), and the proposals that change the format are added by later tickets. It is in the public repository while it is built; until the owner accepts the proposals (`spec/PROCESS.md`), its schema addresses and its pages of the book are not served. Where this text says 0.6, it is the text of 0.6 not yet carried to 0.7.

## 1 Scope

This document specifies the format OSD 0.6: the schemas of the six kinds of descriptor (`device`, `profile`, `sound`, `fingerprint`, `firmware` and `assembly`), their permanent addresses, the conformance cases, and what a reader and a converter return for a descriptor.

The format describes two levels: what a musician handles (controls, roles, ranges, the settings of a sound) and the bytes of a message (frames, expressions, codings). Each technical section says which level it describes.

This document does not specify how an assembly is built, which tool writes a resource to a device, or the catalogue that holds descriptors. Any reader, in any language, needs only the schemas, the conformance cases and this text.

## 2 Conformance

### 2.1 Classes

Four classes conform: a descriptor, a reader, a resolving reader and a converter, each defined in section 4. A reader judges a descriptor alone; a resolving reader also follows its links and judges the needs of a sound; a converter carries a descriptor to a later version.

### 2.2 Keywords and informative content

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT" and "MAY" in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

A requirement with `MUST` or `MUST NOT` is mandatory, one with `SHOULD` or `SHOULD NOT` is recommended, and one with `MAY` is optional. This document binds a class with these five key words only.

A NOTE, an EXAMPLE, a code block and a section whose heading ends in "(informative)" are informative: they carry no key word and bind no class. The text is usable with every informative block removed.

### 2.3 What a reader passes

A reader MUST state the versions it reads.

A reader MUST pass every conformance case of the folders `read`, `coding` and `vocabulary` of each version it states it reads, except the optional cases.

A resolving reader MUST pass every conformance case of the folders `resolve` and `judge_needs` of each version it states it reads, except the optional cases.

A converter MUST pass every conformance case of the folder `convert` of each version it converts to.

The optional cases are the Lua cases and the identity cases: the suite marks them in `conformance/optional.json`.

A reader MAY pass an optional case.

A reader without Lua MUST report to its caller that a descriptor holds Lua, in a form of its own choosing.

A reader without Lua MUST NOT ignore a descriptor that holds Lua silently.

> NOTE The suite checks itself: the examples and the inputs of every conformance case that expects a result validate against the schemas of their version, and a reading case that expects a fault has exactly one schema error, at the pointer it names. The format of a case, and what a reader returns for it, are in [conformance/README.md](../conformance/README.md).

### 2.4 Input that does not conform

A descriptor MUST NOT hold a fault.

A reader MUST refuse a descriptor that holds a fault, with the kind of the fault and the JSON pointer of the faulty place.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a resolution that meets a fault, in the file where the fault arises.

A converter MUST refuse a descriptor that it cannot convert, with `NotConvertible` at the pointer of the element it cannot convert.

A converter MUST NOT return a text for a descriptor that it refuses.

A reader MAY give a fault a name of its own, provided it reports the kind that the case names.

### 2.5 Extensibility

The format is extensible by three mechanisms: a note for people, a key that starts with `_` (5.3); a value `x-<registrant>-<term>` where a list is open (6.2); a field marked `x-osd-extension` in the schemas (5.3).

### 2.6 The schema, the cases and the text

The schema prevails for the shape of a descriptor, the conformance cases for the meaning of it, and the text yields to both; where the text and a schema or a case differ, the difference is a defect of the text, corrected by a correction version (see [ERRATA.md](ERRATA.md)).

The text prevails only for what neither a schema nor a case can express, for example what a reader without Lua reports.

### 2.7 A claim of conformance

A reader, a resolving reader or a converter that claims conformance MUST name the version of the format, its class and the version of the conformance suite.

| resolving reader | a reader that also resolves a driver or an assembly (sections 15 and 23) and judges the needs of a sound (section 16) |
| converter | a program that carries a descriptor of an earlier version to a later one |

| fault | a place where a descriptor does not conform, with a kind and a JSON pointer (RFC 6901) |
| `UnknownField` | a fault: a key that the version does not define |
| `BadValue` | a fault: a value outside its type or its list |
| `Missing` | a fault: a required field that is absent |
| `UnreadVersion` | a fault: a version that the reader does not read |
| `LinkMissing` | a fault: a file named by a link that the catalogue does not hold |
| `LinkCycle` | a fault: links that come back to a file already followed |

## 5 Descriptors

### 5.1 What is published

This section describes the documents of the format, at the level of the musician and of the bytes.

### 5.2 The kinds

| Descriptor | What it says | Schema |
|---|---|---|
| Device | Identity and how to recognise it (identify reply, port names, USB identity, what it sends by itself); controls (CC, 14-bit CC, NRPN, RPN, SysEx parameters); named messages and checksums; programs and banks; drum and sample notes; what it sends back; where each piece of data comes from and how far it was checked | [`device.schema.json`](../schemas/0.6/device.schema.json) |
| Maker profile | What a maker's or a family's devices share (identify request, program scheme), extended by each device | [`profile.schema.json`](../schemas/0.6/profile.schema.json) |

| Fingerprint | What a detection observed of a device plugged in (port names, replies, USB identity, the controls its knobs sent) and the model the person confirmed: recognises devices that do not identify themselves, and teaches the next detection | [`fingerprint.schema.json`](../schemas/0.6/fingerprint.schema.json) |

### 5.3 Reading a descriptor

A descriptor MUST name its version by `$schema`.

A reader MUST refuse a field that the version named by `$schema` does not define, at that field, as `UnknownField`.

A key that starts with `_` is a note for people (`_usage`, `_limitations`). A reader MUST accept it, keep it and write it back as it is, and MUST NOT read a meaning in it. The one exception is `_sources`, which a reader MUST refuse as `BadValue` at `/_sources`, whatever its value, alone or beside `sources`: the credit of a 0.7 descriptor is the field `sources`, and the converter from 0.6 writes the `_sources` of every kind under it.

The converter from 0.6 carries a source as follows. `preset_name` becomes `title` (`name` stays as it is when both are present), `name` alone becomes `title`, `revision` becomes `version`, `content_sha256` becomes `sha256`; when the target key already exists it wins and the old key stays. `preset_id` and `adaptation` are dropped only when the last segment of the path of `url` (the query, the fragment and a final `/` ignored) is exactly their text, with the case, and stay otherwise. `imported_at` becomes the note `_imported_at`, its value unchanged; when that key is taken, `_imported_at_2`, then the next number (5.3.2). A `type` named after a product and a `provider` follow 6.8.4. Every other key is left as it is. The text of a script (`x-osd-extension`) is left as it is, byte for byte. A device's `_midi_of_sources` becomes `midi_of_sources`.

Cases: `0.7/convert/sources-key-renamed`, `0.7/convert/source-product-keys-become-general-fields`, `0.7/convert/script-text-unchanged`, `0.7/read/sources-read`, `0.7/read/underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/underscore-sources-beside-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/underscore-key-is-kept`.

#### The credit of every kind (0.7)

A maker profile, a sound, a fingerprint, a firmware and an assembly of 0.7 MAY carry `sources`, a list of sources of the same definition as a device's, so that every descriptor can name its sources and their licence.

A reader MUST judge each of these sources by the rules of a source of a device (5.3.2, 5.3.4, 6.6 to 6.8) and write it back as it is read.

A reader MUST refuse, in a profile, a sound, a fingerprint, a firmware and an assembly of 0.7, a `_sources` as `BadValue` at `/_sources`, as in a device.

The assembly a sound holds in `made_on` follows the rules of an assembly: it may carry `sources`, and a reader MUST refuse its `_sources` as `BadValue` at `/made_on/_sources`. The sources of a sound and those of its `made_on` are in one file: a reader MUST refuse an `id` written twice among them, at the later source, those of the sound coming first.

Cases: `0.7/read/profile-sources-read`, `0.7/read/profile-source-keys-written-back`, `0.7/read/profile-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/profile-source-product-type-refused`, `0.7/read/sound-sources-read`, `0.7/read/sound-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/sound-source-id-duplicated-refused`, `0.7/read/sound-source-imported-at-refused`, `0.7/read/sound-made-on-sources-read`, `0.7/read/sound-made-on-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/fingerprint-sources-read`, `0.7/read/fingerprint-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/fingerprint-source-license-refused`, `0.7/read/firmware-sources-read`, `0.7/read/firmware-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/firmware-source-provider-malformed-refused`, `0.7/read/firmware-source-provider-unknown-warned`, `0.7/read/assembly-sources-read`, `0.7/read/assembly-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/read/assembly-source-without-type-refused`.

In a source of 0.7, `profile` is a string: the id of the maker profile the source comes from. A resolving reader writes it (23.42).

A reader MUST refuse a `profile` of a source that is not a string, as `BadValue` at the field.

A reader MUST keep a `profile` an author wrote in a source, and MUST NOT refuse it because the catalogue holds no such profile.

Before 0.7 a `profile` in a source is a key kept as it is.

Cases: `0.7/read/source-profile-not-a-string-refused`, `0.7/read/source-profile-of-an-author-kept`.

Before 0.7 `sources` is no field of a profile, a sound, a fingerprint, a firmware or an assembly, and their `_sources` is a note of any value.

A reader of a version before 0.7 MUST refuse `sources` in these descriptors, as `UnknownField` at `/sources`.

Cases: `0.6/read/sound-sources-field-refused`, `0.6/read/sound-underscore-sources-kept`.

Since the `_sources` of these kinds was free, a converter to 0.7 judges it entry by entry, in a profile, a sound, a fingerprint, a firmware, an assembly and the `made_on` of a sound.

A converter MUST write under `sources`, at the same place and in their order, each entry that is a source of 0.7 once carried as a device's (above and 6.8.4).

A converter MUST carry an object entry that has no `type` in the same way, with the `type` `contributed` and `no type in 0.6` as the first line of `notes`, so that its address and licence stay credited.

A converter MUST keep every other entry unchanged (one that is no object, one that a reader of 0.7 refuses even once carried, the later of two entries of one `id` in the file), in a list under the note `_sources_2` (when that key is taken, `_sources_3`, then the next number), without taking the others out of `sources`.

A converter MUST keep a `_sources` that is no list whole under that note.

A converter MUST NOT write `sources` when no entry goes to it, and MUST NOT refuse a descriptor for an entry of its `_sources`, but for the words of a 0.4 profile below.

A converter MUST keep a `profile` that is not a string under `_profile` (when that key is taken, `_profile_2`, then the next number), in every source it carries, a device's included.

From 0.4, a converter first writes the `type` and the `license` of each entry as the tables of 6.6 and 6.7 say them. A converter MUST refuse, as `NotConvertible` at its place under `/_sources`, a word of a profile of 0.4 that those tables do not map; in another kind it judges the entry with the word as it is. The tables write the words of the entries that go to `sources` only: an entry of a profile of 0.4 kept under the note is kept as the text given writes it, its words of 0.4 included. The `_midi_of_sources` of a profile stays a note.

Cases: `0.7/convert/profile-sources-key-renamed`, `0.7/convert/profile-0-4-source-words-become-terms`, `0.7/convert/sound-sources-renamed`, `0.7/convert/sound-made-on-sources-renamed`, `0.7/convert/fingerprint-sources-renamed`, `0.7/convert/firmware-sources-renamed`, `0.7/convert/assembly-sources-renamed`, `0.7/convert/source-without-type-becomes-contributed`, `0.7/convert/sources-entries-kept-as-note`, `0.7/convert/sources-not-a-list-kept-as-note`, `0.7/convert/sources-note-takes-the-next-free-key`, `0.7/convert/source-profile-not-a-string-kept-as-note`.

A source kept under such a note is credited to no one, and that is said.

A reader MUST warn of each entry of a note `_sources_<n>` (`n` from 2) of a profile, a sound, a fingerprint, a firmware, an assembly or the `made_on` of a sound of 0.7, at the entry, and at the note when it is no list.

A reader MUST NOT refuse a descriptor for such a note.

> NOTE This warning is the one meaning a reader reads in a key that starts with `_`, beside the refusal of `_sources`: it reads none of what the note holds.

Cases: `0.7/read/sound-sources-not-carried-warned`, `0.7/read/profile-sources-note-not-a-list-warned`.

A field marked `x-osd-extension` in the schemas (a few Lua expressions and scripts) belongs to [osc-bridge](https://github.com/roomi-fields/osc-bridge). A reader MAY ignore a field marked `x-osd-extension`.

A device file describes one device from one source; a firmware with its own MIDI is its own file, and `midi_of` names the file whose MIDI it answers to unchanged.

> NOTE A program never needs a note for people. The addresses of a descriptor read alike from one device to the next (`/filter/cutoff`, `/program/recall`): the descriptor turns each into the device's own CC, NRPN, note or SysEx.

### 5.3.1 The title and the description of a control or a command (0.7)

An entry of `cc_params` (a control), an entry of `params`, a command and a trigger MAY each carry:

| Field | Type | Says |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | string, optional, never empty | the name a person reads, of what the control or command does or of the physical control that sends it |
| `description` | string in CommonMark (0.28), optional | a description for a person, of the same thing |

A control MAY also carry `value_description` (a string in CommonMark (0.28): what the values of the control mean, beside `values`) and `notes` (a string: a note of reserve or history).

A reader MUST refuse a `title`, a `description` or a `value_description` that is not a string, as `BadValue` at the field, and an empty `title` likewise. A reader draws no structure from a `description` or a `value_description`, and an absent field stays absent: a `title` is optional, and an address is never a name.

A converter from 0.6 moves, on the entries of `cc_params` (`_usage` to `value_description`, `_name` to `title`, `_description` to `description`, `_notes` to `notes`), of `params` and of triggers (`_name` to `title`, `_description` to `description`), the text of the `_` key to its field, unchanged, when the value is a string (not empty for `_name`) and the field is absent. Otherwise the `_` key stays as it is, so that no text is lost and the converted descriptor is valid. A converter writes no `title` for an entry that has only its address, and never copies an address into a `title`. `_usage` never becomes `description`.
### 5.3.2 The removed fields

0.7 removes seven fields: `transform_reverse` (of the entries of `cc_params` and `params`), `device.revision`, `transport.rate_limit_hz` (the `device.rate_limit_hz` stays), `sysex.footer`, `midi_out.note_offset`, `identity.sends` (the `sends` of a fingerprint stays) and `sources[].imported_at`. None of them is a text written for people. `_coverage` is not removed: it is accepted, kept and written back as it is. `capabilities` is not removed either, and is written back as it is, except that the converter removes from it the capability that only a removed field required (`lua` when `transform_reverse` was the last field holding Lua), and the key when the list becomes empty.

A reader MUST refuse a removed field at that field, as `UnknownField`, like every key the version does not define. A source is an open object that keeps its other unknown keys, but a reader MUST refuse `imported_at`, whatever its value, in a source of `sources` and of `midi_of_sources`: the schema of 0.7 declares that key forbidden in every source. The test of the `lua` capability no longer counts `transform_reverse`: a 0.7 device requires `lua` for `transform` and the other fields that hold a script.

The converter from 0.6 drops six of the seven, and only them: it deletes no text written for people and leaves the text of every script as it is, byte for byte. It keeps the seventh as a note: a converter MUST write the value of `imported_at`, unchanged, under the key `_imported_at` of the same source, in `sources` and in `midi_of_sources`. A key `_imported_at` already there stays as it is, and a converter MUST then write the value under `_imported_at_2` (when that key is taken, `_imported_at_3`, then the next number). A 0.6 text that carries the seven still reads as 0.6.

Cases: `0.7/convert/removed-fields-dropped`, `0.7/convert/source-imported-at-becomes-a-note`, `0.7/read/transform-reverse-refused`, `0.7/read/params-transform-reverse-refused`, `0.7/read/device-revision-refused`, `0.7/read/transport-rate-limit-refused`, `0.7/read/sysex-footer-refused`, `0.7/read/midi-out-note-offset-refused`, `0.7/read/identity-sends-refused`, `0.7/read/source-imported-at-refused`, `0.7/read/midi-of-source-imported-at-refused`, `0.7/convert/midi-of-source-imported-at-becomes-a-note`.

### 5.3.3 A control reaches its messages by ways (0.7)

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician. It is the text of proposal 0009 with the contexts of proposal 0003: it replaces the flat fields of a control, `also`, and the `transmit` and `recognize` of the entry, which 22.2, 22.6 and 21.3 say for 0.6.

An entry of `cc_params` (a control) and an entry of `params` has `ways`, a list of zero or more ways. The fields `cc`, `cc_lsb`, `nrpn_msb`, `nrpn_lsb`, `rpn_msb`, `rpn_lsb`, `value_bits` and `also` are no fields of the entry: they live on the ways. So do `range`, `transmit`, `recognize` and `nrpn_lsb_range` on an entry that has a way; an entry with no way (below) carries them itself. The entry keeps what is the control's own: `osc`, `role`, `default`, `priority`, `step_count`, `values`, `expects`, `section`, `transform`, `title`, `description`, `value_description`, `notes`, `polarity`, `channel` and `scale`. A way of a `cc_params` entry may also carry `in` and `slice`, which say the context it holds in (below); a way of a `params` entry does not, and a reader MUST refuse them there as `UnknownField` at `/params/entries/N/ways/M/in` or `/slice`.

A way says one message and how it is reached:

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `ctrlType` | the message kind: `cc`, `nrpn`, `rpn`, `chPress`, `pPress`, `pBend`, `note`, or `sysex` (a `params` entry only) |
| `ctrlIndex` | the numbers of a message of MIDI, a list of integers (below); absent for `chPress`, `pBend` and `sysex` |
| `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r` | on a `sysex` way only: the bytes of the maker's frame, each an integer from 0 to 255 |
| `id` | on a `sysex` way only: the parameter's own number in the maker's frame, any integer, a negative one too |
| `value_bits` | the width of the value, 7 or 14 |
| `range` | lowest and highest value of the message |
| `transmit`, `recognize` | `absolute`, `relative`, `both`, `none`, the four words of M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12 |
| `nrpn_lsb_range` | for an `nrpn` way only: `[lowest, highest]`, the parameter LSB numbers of the block the way stands for (22.4, unchanged) |

| `ctrlType` | `ctrlIndex` |
|---|---|
| `cc` | `[msb]` (7 bits), or `[msb, lsb]` (14 bits, with `value_bits: 14`), each 0 to 127 |
| `nrpn`, `rpn` | `[msb, lsb]`, each 0 to 127 |
| `pPress`, `note` | `[note]`, 0 to 127 |
| `chPress`, `pBend` | none: the field is absent, never an empty list |
| `sysex` | none: its numbers are the fields `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r` and `id` (below) |

**The numbers of a `sysex` way.** A `sysex` way gives each number that addresses the parameter in the maker's frame as a field under its name: `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r`, each an integer from 0 to 255, and `id`, any integer. It gives only those the frame takes, and at least one. It has no `ctrlIndex`.

A reader MUST refuse a `sysex` way that gives none of `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r` and `id`, as `BadValue` at the way. A reader MUST refuse a `ctrlIndex` on a `sysex` way, as `UnknownField` at `ctrlIndex`. A reader MUST refuse `pr`, `p`, `c` or `r` below 0, above 255 or not an integer, and an `id` that is not an integer, as `BadValue` at the field. A reader MUST refuse any of these five fields on a way of a kind of MIDI, as `UnknownField` at the field.

A way with several of these faults MUST be refused for the first in this order: a `ctrlType` its table does not take (`BadValue` at `ctrlType`); a field its kind does not hold (`UnknownField`); a number of a wrong value (`BadValue`); none of the five numbers (`BadValue` at the way). So `{ "ctrlType": "sysex", "p": 16 }` on `cc_params` and a `cc` way that carries `pr` on `params` are `BadValue` at `ctrlType`, and a `sysex` way of `params` with `ctrlIndex` alone is `UnknownField` at `ctrlIndex`.

`values`, `expects`, `section`, `transform` and `role` are not on the way: each says the control, not one message that reaches it. `values` is a list of `{ "value": integer, "title": string }` in the order written, the words of the `ctrlMapId` list of M2-117-UM.

A reader MUST refuse a control without `ways` as `Missing` at `/ways`. A reader MUST accept an empty list, `ways: []`. A reader MUST refuse, at the field and as `UnknownField`, a flat field and `also` on the entry, and `range`, `transmit`, `recognize` or `nrpn_lsb_range` on an entry that has a way. A reader MUST refuse a `ctrlType` that is not one of the eight words, as `BadValue` at `ctrlType`; a `sysex` way on a `cc_params` entry and a way of a kind of MIDI on a `params` entry, as `BadValue` at `ctrlType`; a `ctrlIndex` that does not follow its kind of MIDI, as `BadValue` at `ctrlIndex`, and a `ctrlIndex` present (an empty list too) for `chPress` or `pBend`.

`value_bits`. A reader MUST require it with `nrpn` and `rpn` (`Missing` when absent, `BadValue` for a width other than 7 or 14) and with a `cc` of two numbers (it MUST be 14). A simple `cc` MAY omit it, or give 7: any other width is `BadValue`. `chPress`, `pPress` and `note` carry none; `pBend` is 14 bits and MAY give 14. A reader MUST NOT read the absence of `value_bits` on a simple `cc` as a missing field.

**The scale of a control.** A control MAY give `scale`, `[lowest, highest]`: the range its `default`, `values`, `step_count` and readings are written in, the reference scale. Without it, the reference scale is the range of the most precise way: the way whose maximum is the highest, its `range`, else the range its kind and width imply (0 to 127 for 7 bits, 0 to 16383 for 14); on equal maxima the first way in the written order is the reference. A reader MUST refuse a `default` and a named value outside the reference scale, bounds included, when the scale is known.

**An entry with no way (a partial control).** A description may know a control and not yet the message that reaches it. Its entry gives `ways: []`: the empty list is how it says that the message is unknown, and no other field says it. Such an entry claims no message. It alone may carry on the entry `range` (`[lowest, highest]`), `transmit`, `recognize` and, in `cc_params`, `nrpn_lsb_range`, with the meaning they have on a way; a reader keeps them, and every other field of the entry, as written. Its reference scale is its `scale`, else its `range`. A reader MUST refuse, as `BadValue` at the value, a `default`, a named value and the `range` of a reading outside that scale. An entry with no way that gives neither `scale` nor `range` has no known scale: a reader compares these values to nothing. `nrpn_lsb_range` is never a field of a `params` entry.

A reader MUST warn of each entry of `cc_params` and of `params` that has no way, naming its JSON pointer (`/cc_params/entries/N`, `/params/entries/N`): it is partial, to be completed. A reader MUST NOT refuse a descriptor for such an entry. The same holds for an entry of 0.4, 0.5 or 0.6 that gives no message fully known (in `cc_params`: no `cc`, and no NRPN or RPN given by both its numbers, in its flat fields or in `also`; in `params`: no `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r` or `id`): a reader of this version reads it with no way and the fields its text gives; a message not fully known (below, what the converter writes) gives no way.

A tool converts a value of the reference scale to the scale of a way by the affine scaling of the one range on the other, rounded to the nearest, the half upward, never truncated:

`way = way_min + round((v − ref_min) × (way_max − way_min) / (ref_max − ref_min))`

and the same formula, the other way round, for a value received on a way. For ranges that start at 0 it is `round(v × way_max / ref_max)`. With the reference `[-64, 63]` and a way `[0, 127]`: −64 gives 0, 0 gives 64, 63 gives 127. With a 14-bit reference, a `default` of 8192 is 64 on a 7-bit way (8192 × 127 / 16383 is about 63.5, rounded up) and stays 8192 on the 14-bit way.

**The side of a direction.** `transmit` and `recognize` are read from the point of view of the device or the software the descriptor describes, as the controller list of M2-117-UM v1.0 (tables 6 and 12) says them of a device: `transmit` other than `none`, it sends the message; `recognize` other than `none`, it receives it.

**The order of the ways.** A reader MUST give the ways in the order written and MUST NOT reorder them. A tool that sends to the device takes the first way it can write whose `recognize` is not `none`; a tool that listens to the device recognises every way whose `transmit` is not `none`. A way with `none` for both claims nothing. A way with neither `transmit` nor `recognize` written says nothing of its direction: it works on both sides and claims on both. Only an explicit `none` removes a side.

**The message claimed once, by side.** A way claims its message on each side where it works (the side where the device sends it, when `transmit` is not `none`; the side where the device receives it, when `recognize` is not `none`), and the numbers it occupies: `[7]` claims the CC 7, `[7, 39]` claims the CC numbers 7 and 39; an NRPN or an RPN `[msb, lsb]` is one message; `[note]` claims the note. Two ways claim the same message when they have the same `ctrlType`, a shared claimed number (for `cc`; for the other kinds, the same numbers), the same channel (or both none) and claim on one same side. A `note` and a `pPress` of one number are two messages. A reader MUST refuse the later claim as `BadValue` at the later way (`/cc_params/entries/N/ways/M`), and the rule MUST name the earlier way by the same form of pointer, across the ways of a control as across the controls. `nrpn_lsb_range` claims only the message of its own `ctrlIndex`.

**The contexts a message holds in.** A device may declare `contexts`, an object: each key a dimension (a mode, a set of parts), its `values` a list of objects with an `id` (a text that is not empty) and an optional `channel` (0 to 15). A value without a `channel` has a channel that is the user's setting: the reader never compares it, and trusts the parts of a dimension to separate. Two values of one dimension that both write a `channel` MUST NOT write the same one: the reader refuses the second as `BadValue` at `/contexts/<dimension>/values/N/channel`; a value with an `id` missing is refused at `/contexts/<dimension>/values/N/id`. A way of `cc_params` may carry `in`, an object whose keys are dimensions of the device and whose values are one id or a list of ids, and `slice`, `[lowest, highest]`, a closed interval of two integers with lowest at most highest, bounds included, not checked against `value_bits` or `range`. A dimension or a value that `in` names and the device does not declare, an `in` of a wrong form and a `slice` that is not such an interval are `BadValue` at their pointer.

**The one rule of claims.** A claim is a message (its kind, its channel, its numbers, as above) with its coordinates: for each dimension its way names in `in`, the set of ids, and, when it has a `slice`, the interval, under the one name `value`. Two claims of one message hold together unless a coordinate named by both has two sets with nothing in common; a coordinate that only one names leaves them together, and a way with no `in` and no `slice` holds in every context. A reader MUST refuse the later claim as above when the two hold together, and admit it when they cannot. A mode, a part and a slice are not three rules: each is a coordinate, and the one test (do the two sets meet) decides it. Two slices that share a bound meet (`[0, 63]` and `[63, 127]`); `[0, 63]` and `[64, 127]` do not. A `midi_of` file read alone, with no `contexts` of its own, does not hold the dimensions of its target: it keeps each `in` as written, checks only its shape, and compares two ways on the coordinates whose dimension it knows (its own `contexts`, and `value`); a coordinate of an unknown dimension is not compared and separates nothing, and the names are checked by the reading that joins the file and its target. A file of 0.6 or before has no `contexts`, `in` or `slice`: every claim has no coordinate and the rule is the one of those versions.

**What the converter writes.** For a 0.6 control, the converter writes: a way for each message fully known, the first from the flat fields (`cc` to `ctrlType: cc` and `ctrlIndex: [cc]`, with `cc_lsb` `[cc, cc_lsb]` and `value_bits: 14`; the pairs of an NRPN or an RPN to `[msb, lsb]`), each element of `also` as the next way, in order, with its own `range` and `value_bits`, a message not fully known giving none; `range` on its way, and `nrpn_lsb_range` as said below; `transmit` and `recognize` of the entry on every way, only when the entry had them; `values`, a dictionary, as the list of `{ value, title }` in the order of the dictionary; the other fields of the entry unchanged. For a `params` entry it writes one way, `ctrlType: sysex`, with each of `pr`, `p`, `c`, `r` and `id` that the entry gives, under the same name and with the same value (an `id` of any sign and size), and its `range`, `transmit` and `recognize`, the rest staying on the entry. A note of the entry stays as written. It writes `scale`, the range of the first way, only when the control has a `default`, `values` or `step_count` and another way has a strictly higher maximum than the first (a `range` that no way takes, or a first message not fully known, gives the `scale` said below instead); it never rewrites `default`, `values` (but for their list form) or `step_count`. A converter MUST keep the order of the ways and MUST NOT join two controls; it writes no value its source lacks. An entry of `cc_params` or `params` that gives no message fully known is written with `ways: []`: its `range`, `transmit`, `recognize` and `nrpn_lsb_range` stay on the entry, unchanged, the converter writes no `scale`, no note but those said below, and the other fields convert as those of any control. A control of 0.4 or 0.5 whose flat fields mix kinds of message, which 0.6 refuses, is written as ways by the rules of 21.3.

**The block and the range of a control that has ways.** `nrpn_lsb_range` is a field of an `nrpn` way. For an entry of `cc_params` of 0.4, 0.5 or 0.6 that gives it and has a way in 0.7, a converter MUST write it on one `nrpn` way and on no other: the way of the flat fields when they give both `nrpn_msb` and `nrpn_lsb`; else, when they give neither, the single `nrpn` way written from `also`. When no way can receive it (the flat fields give one number of an NRPN alone; or they give none, and `also` gives no `nrpn` way or several), a converter MUST NOT refuse the entry for it and MUST NOT choose a way: it MUST keep the value, unchanged, on the entry under `_nrpn_lsb_range`, a note for people; when that key is taken, under `_nrpn_lsb_range_2`, `_nrpn_lsb_range_3` and so on, the first free. No way takes the `range` of an entry of 0.6 that gives it, no flat message field and a non-empty `also`, and has a way in 0.7: it would have to be moved, and an entry that has a way carries none. A converter MUST NOT refuse the entry for it and MUST NOT write it on a way: it MUST keep the value, unchanged, on the entry under `_range`, a note for people; when that key is taken, under `_range_2`, `_range_3` and so on, the first free. A way of such an entry has a `range` only when its element of `also` gave one. When the entry has a `default`, `values` or `step_count`, which 0.6 writes in that `range` (0.6, section 22.1), the reference scale of the 0.7 control MUST be that `range`: a converter MUST write it as `scale` when the most precise way has another scale, whatever the scale of the first way, and MUST NOT write a `scale` otherwise. Without these three fields it writes no `scale`: nothing of the control is written in that `range`, kept for people alone in `_range`, and the reference scale is that of the ways. When such an entry has no way, because no element of its `also` is a message fully known, its `range` stays on the entry, as for any entry with no way, with no `_range` and no `scale`. A control of 0.4 or 0.5 that mixes kinds of message follows 21.3, whose refusal, of an `nrpn_lsb_range` without an NRPN too, stays at the entry, `/cc_params/entries/N`. These rules are those of the conversion to 0.7 alone, by a converter or by a resolution that carries a file to 0.7: a reading, a writing in its own version and a conversion to a version before 0.7 keep these fields as given.

**A message not fully known.** An NRPN or an RPN given by one of its two numbers, and a `cc_lsb` without its `cc`, are messages not fully known: a way says an NRPN or an RPN by `[msb, lsb]`, and `[n]` of a `cc` is the CC n. For such a message of an entry of `cc_params` of 0.4, 0.5 or 0.6, a converter MUST NOT write a way, MUST NOT add a number and MUST NOT refuse the entry for it: the messages fully known of the entry become its ways as above, and a message not fully known claims nothing. A converter MUST keep the messages not fully known on the entry under `_incomplete`, a note for people (when that key is taken, under `_incomplete_2`, `_incomplete_3` and so on, the first free; a key the text wrote stays as it is): a list of one object per such message, in the order of the source, the flat fields then `also`, with the numbers the text gives and no other, `value_bits` when the text gives it and, for an element of `also`, its `range` and its `_` notes. The `range` of the flat fields goes into the object of their message only when the entry keeps a way; else it stays on the entry, with `transmit`, `recognize` and `nrpn_lsb_range`, as for any entry with no way. When the first message of the source is not fully known, a way remains and the control has a `default`, `values` or `step_count`, the reference scale stays that of this first message (its `range`, else the one its width implies, 0 to 127 for a `cc_lsb`): the converter MUST write it as `scale` when the ways would give another reference without it, and rewrites none of these values. A reading of a text of 0.4, 0.5 or 0.6 gives such a message no way either, and a writing in the version of the text gives its fields back as they were. ### 5.3.4 The readings of a command (0.7)

This section is the text of proposal 0006 for a command (the readings of a control are in 5.3.6). A command MAY carry `readings`, a non-empty array of objects:

| Field | Type | Says |
|---|---|---|
| `state` | one of `valid`, `to-confirm`, `refuted`, required | what the reading is worth, as the service that wrote the descriptor holds it |
| `firmware` | string, required | the firmware the reading holds for; `unknown` when it is not known |
| `source` | string, optional | the `id` of the source that gave the reading |
| `title` | string, optional, never empty | the title of the command in this reading |
| `args` | array of arguments, optional | the arguments of the command in this reading, of the form of `args` of a command |

A reading says only what two accounts of one command can differ on: for the reading, its `title` and `args` replace those of the command, and the address and the section stay on the command. The three words are ranks, as Wikidata ranks a statement (preferred, normal, deprecated): `valid` may be used as the command, `to-confirm` may be used and a tool that shows it says it is not confirmed, `refuted` is kept as a record and is not offered by default. A reader passes the state on and never computes it. A command without `readings` is its own reading, with no state.

Each entry of `sources` and of `midi_of_sources` MAY carry `id`, a string that is not empty. The `id` of the sources of a file are unique; for a file with `midi_of` the `id` of its sources and of its target's form one set where each is unique (23.4). The format reserves no `id`. Before 0.7 an `id` in `_sources` is a key kept as it is, never checked.

The `source` of a reading, of a command or of a control, MUST name a source by its `id`; which entries count depends on how the file is read.

In a file without `midi_of` read without being resolved, the `source` of a reading, of a command or of a control, MUST be an `id` of an entry written in its `sources` or its `midi_of_sources`, an entry that carries `profile` included (a complete descriptor read again alone, 23.42).

A reader MUST NOT refuse a `midi_of` file read alone because the `source` of a reading names no `id` of its own lists: its target may hold it, and the resolution judges it.

A resolving reader MUST judge a file without `midi_of`, the end of a `midi_of` chain included, on its own text before it joins it, as read without being resolved (23.33): the `source` of each of its readings, kept or discarded by the join, MUST be an `id` of an entry of its own `sources` or `midi_of_sources`, and the reader MUST refuse one that is not, as `BadValue`, in that file, at the pointer of that `source` in it.

For the readings of the files with `midi_of`, a resolving reader MUST judge, on the joined device of the file asked for, the `source` of each such reading that device keeps against the `id` of the `sources` and `midi_of_sources` written in all the files joined (the file asked for and its targets), and MUST refuse a `source` that names none of them, as `BadValue`, in the file asked for, at the pointer of that `source` in the joined device. The sources the resolution appends from profiles (23.42) are not in that set. A reading of a `midi_of` file that the join discards, because its command or control is replaced at the same address, is not judged.

A reader MUST refuse: a reading without `state` or without `firmware`, as `Missing` at that key; a `state` outside the three words, as `BadValue` at `state`; an empty `readings`, an empty `title` of a reading, an empty or twice-written `id` (at the second entry), and a `source` that names no `id` (but not in a `midi_of` file read alone, which the resolution judges), as `BadValue`; a key a reading does not define, as `UnknownField`; and `readings` in a descriptor of 0.4 to 0.6, as `UnknownField`.

A reader gives the readings of a command as they are written, `title` and `args` raw. For a firmware asked, it gives the readings whose `firmware` is the same text, `valid` then `to-confirm` then, when asked, `refuted`, each state in the order written. When that list is empty, because no reading has that firmware or the only ones are `refuted` and not asked, it gives all the readings of the command that are not `refuted` (all of them when `refuted` is asked), in the order written, none chosen: a firmware is free text, and the format does not say that a reading of 1.2 holds on 1.3.

The converter from 0.6 writes no `readings` and leaves every `id` as it is.

### 5.3.5 The changes of form (0.7)

0.7 says three facts in one form, where 0.6 said them in another. Each is the text of proposal 0008, section 2.

**`polarity`.** A control (an entry of `cc_params`) MAY carry `polarity`, one of `unipolar` (the lowest value is the start, as a level) or `bipolar` (the middle value is the rest position, as a pan), the words of MIDI 2.0 for unipolar and bipolar values. It replaces `orientation`, which is no field of 0.7: a reader MUST refuse it as `UnknownField`, and refuse a `polarity` of any other word as `BadValue` at the field. The converter from 0.6 writes `bipolar` for `centered` and `unipolar` for `0-based`; an absent `orientation` stays absent and gets no `polarity`; any other value is `NotConvertible` at `/cc_params/entries/<n>/orientation`.

**`identity.manufacturerId`.** The maker's SysEx id, a list of bytes (0 to 127), is `manufacturerId` of the block `identity`, the name of the DeviceInfo of MIDI 2.0 Property Exchange; `device.manufacturer_id` is no field of 0.7 and a reader MUST refuse it as `UnknownField`. `device.device_id` stays in `device`. The key is a key directly in `identity`, so 23.25 applies: `null` is accepted, and drops the profile's `manufacturerId` from the resolved description; a `null` on a profile that does not give it is accepted. A file with `midi_of` that has an `identity` block replaces its target's block whole, `manufacturerId` included (23.8); without a block it takes its target's. The converter from 0.6 moves `device.manufacturer_id` to `identity.manufacturerId`, the same bytes, when the file has no `midi_of` or has one and an `identity` block; a file with `midi_of` and no `identity` block gets no block (it would replace its target's whole): its value stays as the note `device._manufacturer_id`, kept and written back.

**`id` on a command.** A command MAY carry `id`, an integer: the number a controller's preset gives it. Two commands MAY carry the same `id`; a reader refuses none. A reader MUST refuse an `id` that is not an integer as `BadValue` at the field. The converter from 0.6 writes `id` from an `_electra` whose `parameter` is of `type` `sysex` with an integer `number` `N`, the number to the byte. It removes that `_electra` when it is exactly `{"parameter": {"type": "sysex", "number": N}}`; an `_electra` that carries one more key, beside `parameter` or inside it, gives `id` and stays whole as a note under `_electra`, no value changed. An `_electra` of any other shape (the `virtual` form, another `type`, a `number` that is not an integer, no `parameter`) stays as a note under `_electra`, kept and written back, with no `id`.

### 5.3.6 The readings of a control (0.7)

This section is the text of proposal 0006 for a control. A `cc_params` entry and a `params` entry MAY carry `readings`, a non-empty array of objects of the form of a command's reading (5.3.4), without `args`, which has no meaning on a control:

| Key | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `state` | one of `valid`, `to-confirm`, `refuted`, required | what the reading is worth |
| `firmware` | string, required | the firmware the reading holds for; `unknown` when it is not known |
| `source` | string, optional | the `id` of the source that gave the reading |
| `title` | string, optional, never empty | the title of the control in this reading |
| `range` | `[lowest, highest]`, two integers, optional | the range of the control in this reading, of the form of `scale` |

The `range` of a reading is written on the reference scale of the control (its `scale`, else the scale of its most precise way, or, with no way, its `range`), as `default` is, and lies inside it, bounds included, its lowest not above its highest. A reader MUST refuse a `range` outside the scale, or with its lowest above its highest, as `BadValue` at the `range` of the reading; when the scale is not known (a `params` entry whose ways give none, an entry with no way and neither `scale` nor `range`), the `range` is not checked. A firmware that produces a wider range than the scale is written by giving the control a wider `scale`, never by a `range` outside it.

A reader MUST refuse: a reading without `state` or without `firmware`, as `Missing` at that key; a `state` outside the three words, an empty `readings`, an empty `title` and a `source` that names no `id` of the sources (5.3.4, which says in which file it is judged), as `BadValue`; a key a reading does not define, `args` included, as `UnknownField`; and `readings` on a control of 0.4 to 0.6, as `UnknownField` at `/cc_params/entries/N/readings` or `/params/entries/N/readings`.

A reader gives the readings of a control as they are written, `title` and `range` raw, never merged with the control's, and writes them back key for key. For a firmware asked it gives the readings by the rule of a command's (5.3.4); a control without `readings` gives none. The converter from 0.6 writes no `readings` on a control.

### 5.3.7 The rules on port names and the signs of MIDI 2.0 (0.7)

This section is the text of proposal 0004. It describes how a device is recognised, at the level of the musician.

#### The rule on a port name

From 0.7, each entry of `identity.ports`, of a device or of a maker profile, is an object, a rule, and no text:

| Key | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `operator` | one of `equals`, `contains`, `starts_with`, `ends_with`, required | how the value is compared with a name |
| `value` | string, not empty, required | the text compared, as it is: no pattern language lives in it |
| `direction` | one of `input`, `output`, optional | the ports the rule names; absent, both directions |
| `platform` | one of `windows`, `linux`, `macos`, optional | the rule holds for ports named on this platform only; absent, everywhere |
| `stack` | one of the stacks below, optional | the rule holds for ports named by this stack only |

The stacks form a closed list grown by version, each belonging to one platform:

| Stack | Platform | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `winmm` | `windows` | the Windows multimedia MIDI functions | Microsoft Learn, "MIDI Reference" (learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/multimedia/midi-reference) |
| `windows-midi-services` | `windows` | Windows MIDI Services, the MIDI 2.0 stack | Microsoft, Windows MIDI Services documentation (microsoft.github.io/MIDI) |
| `winrt` | `windows` | the Windows Runtime MIDI API (`Windows.Devices.Midi`) | Microsoft Learn, "Windows.Devices.Midi Namespace" (learn.microsoft.com/uwp/api/windows.devices.midi) |
| `alsa-seq` | `linux` | the ALSA sequencer | ALSA library reference, "MIDI Sequencer" (alsa-project.org/alsa-doc/alsa-lib/seq.html) |
| `alsa-rawmidi` | `linux` | the ALSA raw MIDI interface | ALSA library reference, "RawMidi Interface" (alsa-project.org/alsa-doc/alsa-lib/rawmidi.html) |
| `jack` | `linux` | the MIDI ports of the JACK audio connection kit | JACK API documentation, `jack/midiport.h` (jackaudio.org/api) |
| `coremidi` | `macos` | Core MIDI | Apple, "Core MIDI" (developer.apple.com/documentation/coremidi) |

A rule with a `stack` MUST name the `platform` the stack belongs to.

A reader MUST refuse, as `BadValue` at the key: an `operator`, a `direction`, a `platform` or a `stack` outside its list; an empty `value`; a `stack` without a `platform` or of another platform than the `platform` named, at `stack`.

A reader MUST refuse an entry of `identity.ports` written as a text, as `BadValue` at `/identity/ports/N`.

A reader MUST refuse a rule without `operator` or without `value`, as `Missing`, and a key a rule does not define, as `UnknownField`.

`ports` and `endpoint_name` are keys directly in `identity`, so 23.25 applies to them: a reader MUST accept `null` on each, which drops the profile's key.

#### The two signs of MIDI 2.0

The block `identity` MAY hold `endpoint_name`, a rule on the name the device gives its own UMP endpoint (M2-104-UM, Endpoint Name Notification): `operator` and `value`, read as those of a port rule. An endpoint serves both directions and the device gives its name itself, so the rule has no `direction`, `platform` or `stack`: a reader MUST refuse any other key of it as `UnknownField`.

A fingerprint MAY hold four fields, each absent when unknown:

| Field | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `platform` | one of the platforms above | the platform the observation was taken on; in a browser, the host's |
| `stack` | one of the stacks above | the naming stack, when the tool knows it |
| `endpoint_name` | string | the endpoint name observed, lower case as the port names are |
| `product_instance_id` | string | the product instance id observed (M2-104-UM, Product Instance Id Notification), which tells two units of one model apart |

A reader MUST refuse a `stack` of a fingerprint without its `platform`, or of another platform, as `BadValue` at `/stack`.

A reader MUST refuse `identity.product_instance_id` in a device or a profile, as `UnknownField`: the id names a unit, not a model.

A reader of 0.4 to 0.6 MUST refuse `identity.endpoint_name` and these four fields of a fingerprint as `UnknownField`, and a rule written as an object in `identity.ports` as `BadValue` at `/identity/ports/N`.

#### The matching of a rule against a fingerprint

A reader that judges an identity gives one verdict per rule, each entry of `identity.ports` in its order and then `identity.endpoint_name`, with the JSON pointer of the rule: `matched`, `not_matched`, or `not_applied` when the rule says nothing of the fingerprint, which is no error. Whether a rule of ports applies to the system of the fingerprint is decided by four statements:

1. A reader that judges an identity MUST apply a rule with no `platform` to every fingerprint, one with no `platform` included.
2. It MUST apply a rule with a `platform` and no `stack` only to a fingerprint that names the same platform, whatever its stack, or none.
3. It MUST apply a rule with a `platform` and a `stack` only to a fingerprint that names the same platform and the same stack.
4. It MUST NOT apply a rule to a fingerprint that lacks the platform or the stack the rule names.

An applied rule compares its `value` with the names of the fingerprint's list of its `direction` (`ports.inputs` or `ports.outputs`), or, with no `direction`, with the names of every list present; one name that suits it is enough, and a list present with no name that suits it, empty or alone, gives `not_matched`. The verdict is `not_applied` when the fingerprint lacks the list of the rule's direction, lacks both lists for a rule with no direction, or lacks `endpoint_name` for the rule on the endpoint name.

A reader that judges an identity MUST compare the two texts under the simple case folding of Unicode (the `C` and `S` mappings of `CaseFolding.txt`), without normalisation: `K` and the Kelvin sign are equal, `ß` and `SS` differ, and a composed letter differs from the same letter followed by its combining mark.

A rule read from a text before 0.7 whose value is empty is never `matched`: applied, it gives `not_matched`.

#### Two product instance ids

A reader that compares two fingerprints by their `product_instance_id` MUST compare the two texts exactly, case included, and answer `same`, `different`, or `unknown` when one of the two holds none or an empty one. The answer reads no other field and does not say that the two fingerprints were taken from one unit: that judgement stays with the detection.

#### What a descriptor before 0.7 becomes

A name of `identity.ports` before 0.7 means that the port name holds it, any case: a reader gives it as a `contains` rule with no `direction`, `platform` or `stack`, and writes the text back unchanged. A converter MUST write each name of `identity.ports`, of a device or a profile, as `{"operator": "contains", "value": <name>}`, in the same order and with no other key. A converter MUST drop an empty name, which recognises nothing, and keep the list, empty when it held no other name. A converter MUST keep a `ports` of `null`. A converter MUST NOT write `endpoint_name` or `product_instance_id`: a fingerprint changes by its `$schema` alone.

The order in which a detection reads its signs, the ranking of two descriptors that match, and any score are not rules of the format.

### 5.3.8 The transport of a software (0.7)

This section describes how a software is reached. A `device` block MAY carry `transport`. Without it, the software is reached by MIDI.

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `kind` | the way the software is reached, `midi` or `osc`; it decides how the commands of the device are sent: by their `frame` in `midi`, by their `forward` in `osc` |

### 5.4 The permanent address of a schema

## 6 Closed vocabularies (0.5)

### 6.1 Closed lists

This section describes the words of five values at the level of the musician.

Five values of a 0.5 descriptor take their word from a closed list, each a set of defined terms. This section is the one place where the lists and the tables are written.

A reader MUST refuse a value outside a closed list, as `BadValue`, except an extension (6.2).

A converter MUST map the makers' words of 0.4 to the terms, by the tables of this section.

A converter MUST keep a 0.4 term that 0.5 keeps.

A converter MUST refuse a word outside the tables, as `NotConvertible`, at the pointer of the value.

A converter MUST NOT guess a word.

A converter MUST NOT normalise the case of a word.

### 6.2 Extension

A value outside a list is valid as `x-<registrant>-<term>`, matching `^x-[a-z0-9]+-[a-z0-9-]+$`, in the lists that are open: not `decode`, which has no extension, and not the `kind` of a 0.4 or 0.5 sound, which stays closed.

A reader MUST accept an extension in an open list.

A reader MUST treat an unknown extension as the default of its field:

- `selects`: a plain recall;
- the `kind` of a 0.6 sound: a `sound`;
- `expects`: `continuous`;
- `sources[].type`: the lowest reliability.

> NOTE The form is on the model of MIDI-CI's `X-` manufacturer resources (M2-103 ResourceList), with a registrant added. [`schemas/extensions.md`](../schemas/extensions.md) lists extensions, each with a one-sentence definition and a stable URL; being listed is required to become a core term in a later version, and the converter then maps it. This departs from RFC 6648, which retired the `X-` prefix for protocol parameters: a closed list that a registry grows by version, with no central authority to ask, needs a prefix that tells a reader at once that a word is not core, and the registrant says whose it is.

### 6.7 `license`

This section carries [proposal 0024](proposals/0024-a-licence-is-an-spdx-expression.md). A `license` (a source's, a sound's, a firmware's) is an SPDX licence expression (SPDX 2.3, annex "SPDX License Expressions"), or one of `public-domain`, `NOASSERTION` and `NONE` alone. Before 0.7 it is one SPDX id or one of these three values.

A descriptor MUST write a `license` as an SPDX licence expression, `public-domain`, `NOASSERTION` or `NONE`.

The grammar of an expression:

```
expression  = and-term *( "OR" and-term )
and-term    = term *( "AND" term )
term        = simple [ "WITH" exception ] / "(" expression ")"
simple      = license-id / "DocumentRef-" idstring ":" "LicenseRef-" idstring
license-id  = idstring, in which "+" is also admitted after the first character
exception   = idstring
idstring    = 1*( letter / digit / "." / "-" )
```

Only the space (U+0020) separates, one or more. `WITH` takes a space on each side. `AND` and `OR` take on each side a space or a parenthesis (`(MIT)OR(ISC)`), and a parenthesis MAY have spaces inside it. Parentheses nest and are balanced. No space stands at either end. `AND`, `OR` and `WITH` are neither an id nor an exception. `WITH` binds tighter than `AND`, and `AND` tighter than `OR`.

A descriptor MUST NOT write `public-domain`, `NOASSERTION` or `NONE`, whatever its case, as an operand or an exception of an expression.

A reader MUST refuse, as `BadValue` at the field, a `license` that is neither a well-formed expression nor one of the three special values.

A reader MUST give back an expression as it is written.

A descriptor MUST write `NOASSERTION` and `NONE` in the SPDX canonical case.

A reader MUST check the shape of an expression, and in it the shape of an SPDX id: letters, digits, `.` and `-`, the first character included, and `+` after it.

A reader MUST NOT check an SPDX id or an exception against the SPDX registry.

A reader MUST refuse another spelling of the special values (`none`, `None`, `Noassertion`, `Public-Domain`).

A converter MUST write `NONE` for a 0.4 `none`.

A converter MUST keep another admitted value of 0.4.

> NOTE The two special values are taken with their SPDX meaning. The text departs from SPDX, which compares them without regard to case: each special value has one spelling.

> NOTE As in SPDX, two spellings of an id that differ only in case name the same licence, and a reader gives each back as it is written. A descriptor writes an id as the SPDX list spells it (`curl`, `MIT`): this is advice that no reader checks, since none reads the list.

> NOTE The table of 0.4 to 0.5 (6.6) keeps the shape of an id from before 0.7: an id that does not start with a capital, in a field the table judges, stays `NotConvertible` at the pointer of the field. A field it does not judge is carried to 0.7 with its id as written.

> NOTE The other departures from SPDX: operators are written in upper case only, and the special values stand alone, never inside an expression (SPDX 3 differs on both); a schema checks the shape by a pattern, which cannot count parentheses, so a reader alone refuses unbalanced ones.

> NOTE A licence expression is a structure inside a string, which rule R8 of the specification's frame (`docs/CADRE.md`) refuses. This text departs from R8 for `license`: it keeps the SPDX string, the form Cargo and npm write, so that a source writes its licence as it states it elsewhere.

Cases: `0.7/read/license-with-exception-accepted`, `0.7/read/license-and-or-parentheses-accepted`, `0.7/read/license-expression-malformed-refused`, `0.7/read/license-parenthesis-unclosed-refused`, `0.7/read/license-special-value-in-expression-refused`, `0.7/read/license-lowercase-id-accepted`

### 6.8 `sources[].provider` and the table of providers (0.7)

This section is the text of proposal 0010.

#### 6.8.1 The field

A source of `sources` and of `midi_of_sources` MAY carry `provider`, the identifier of an entry of the table of providers: who supplies the source. It stands beside `type` and replaces no term of evidence.

An identifier matches `^[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9]+)*$` (lowercase letters and digits in groups joined by single hyphens) and has at most 64 characters. A schema checks this form only and holds no list of identifiers.

Before 0.7 a `provider` in `_sources` is a key kept as it is, never checked.

#### 6.8.2 What a reader does

A reader MUST refuse a `provider` that is not a string, does not match the form or has more than 64 characters, as `BadValue` at the field.

A reader MUST accept a well-formed identifier that its copy of the table does not hold, and give it back as it is written.

A reader MUST warn of such an identifier, naming it.

A reader MUST NOT refuse a descriptor for such an identifier.

A reader MAY give the name and the address of an entry its table holds.

A reader MUST state the version of the table it carries.

&gt; NOTE The table grows by contribution and readers carry different versions of it: a descriptor that cites a recent entry reads everywhere. This departs from the closed lists of 6.1, as the SPDX License List does for its readers. The table says who an entry is, never how far to trust it: a level of trust belongs to a catalogue, keyed by `provider` and `type`.

Cases: `0.7/read/source-provider-known-read`, `0.7/read/source-provider-unknown-accepted`, `0.7/read/source-provider-malformed-refused`, `0.7/read/source-provider-not-a-string-refused`, `0.7/read/source-provider-too-long-refused`

#### 6.8.3 The table

The table is the data file `schemas/providers.json`, beside `schemas/extensions.md`, at the permanent address `https://w3id.org/osd/providers`; an entry has the URL `https://w3id.org/osd/providers#<id>`. It is part of the text of no version of the format.

It is JSON that its schema, `schemas/providers.schema.json`, checks: an object with `version`, the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) of its last change, and `providers`, the array of entries.

| Key of an entry | Holds |
|---|---|
| `id`, required | the identifier, of the form of 6.8.1; never reused |
| `name`, required | the name as its owner writes it |
| `kind`, required | `maker`, `community-firmware` or `partner` |
| `url`, required | the web address of the provider |
| `midi_manufacturer_id` | the MIDI manufacturer id when one exists: one byte as two hexadecimal digits, or three bytes `00 xx xx` as six |
| `credit` | on a seeded entry, where the identifier, the name and the address were read, and the licence read there |
| `deprecated` | the provider has ceased to exist, or the entry was a mistake |
| `replaced_by` | the identifier of the entry that replaces a deprecated one |

A `maker` made the device or its software; a `community-firmware` is an alternative firmware made by people other than the maker; a `partner` is a project whose data formats or catalogues OSD carries. The list of kinds is closed: a new kind is a proposal. Two entries never carry the same `midi_manufacturer_id`, except that a community firmware may carry the id of the maker whose device it runs on.

**Registration.** Anyone may ask for an addition by pull request. A request gives the identifier, the name, the kind, the web address, the MIDI manufacturer id when it exists with the place where it was read, and for a `community-firmware` or a `partner` one line saying what it supplies. The maintainers of the table review each request, check the entry at its source (for an id, the MIDI Association's published table) and merge it when the request is complete, the identifier is free and well formed, and the name is the provider's own. A request is refused with its reason written in the pull request. An entry is never deleted and its `id` is never given to another provider: it stays with `deprecated` and, when another entry replaces it, `replaced_by`. An entry is added when a descriptor needs it. An id of the MIDI Association is restated as a fact, with its source in `credit`: the table copies no MIDI norm.

**Version.** The table has its own version, the date, apart from the versions of the format: an addition makes no new version of the format, and a descriptor of any version that has `provider` cites the same table.

#### 6.8.4 What the converter from 0.6 writes

A converter MUST write by this table each source of `_sources`, and each source of the `_midi_of_sources` of a device only, the results under `sources` and `midi_of_sources` (the `_midi_of_sources` of a profile stays a note, 5.3):

| `type` in 0.6 | `type` in 0.7 | `provider` | `notes` |
|---|---|---|---|
| a term of 6.6, or an extension | the same | none written | unchanged |
| `electra-preset` | `contributed` | `electra-one` | first line `type in 0.6: electra-preset` |
| `pencilresearch` | `contributed` | `pencilresearch` | first line `type in 0.6: pencilresearch` |
| `knobkraft` | `contributed` | `knobkraft` | first line `type in 0.6: knobkraft` |
| `midnam` | `contributed` | none written: a format, not a supplier | first line `type in 0.6: midnam` |

The text that `notes` held follows that first line unchanged; a source without `notes` gets that line alone.

A source of 0.6 has no field `provider` and may carry the key. A converter MUST keep a well-formed string, which wins over the identifier of the table above. A converter MUST keep any other value under `_provider` (when that key is taken, `_provider_2`, then the next number), the identifier of the table above taking its place.

The rule holds from 0.4 and 0.5, through 0.6, and in a resolution that joins a file or a target of 0.6 to a file of 0.7 (23.22).

Cases: `0.7/convert/source-product-types-become-contributed`, `0.7/convert/source-evidence-type-unchanged`, `0.7/convert/source-provider-already-there`, `0.7/convert/source-product-keys-become-general-fields`

## 7 Versions

This section describes the versions of the format and how a descriptor goes from one to the next, at both levels.

A version number names a fixed set of schemas: a published version never changes, a later one lives beside it. `schemas/SHA256SUMS` records the SHA-256 of every published schema file.

> NOTE `cargo test -p osd` fails, naming the file, when a published schema file changes, disappears, or joins a published version. A new version is recorded when it is published, from the `schemas` folder: `sha256sum <version>/*.schema.json >> SHA256SUMS`.

Version 0.6 is published and frozen. [Annex A](#annex-a-what-06-changes-informative) lists every change from 0.5, with what a 0.5 file becomes. Its six files and the vocabulary folder `vocabulary/1` are in the checksums.

Version 0.5 is published and frozen. A converter MUST convert every 0.4 descriptor to 0.5 as this list says. A converter MUST refuse, as `NotConvertible` at its JSON pointer, an element it cannot turn into a structure. A converter MUST return a 0.5 text as it is. Field by field:

- `$schema`: the first key, an address under `https://w3id.org/osd/0.5/` (permanent), in place of the SynthHub one; required in 0.5.
- Frame elements: each `{placeholder}` string of a frame (`{value:u14_lsb}`, `{checksum}`, …) becomes a structure; `{checksum}` without an algorithm and `{function}` are `NotConvertible` (8.3).
- `pre`: an expression string becomes a tree of operations (8.3).
- Addresses: a string with `{name}` becomes a list of segments, text or capture; a brace that is not one name is `NotConvertible` (9.4).
- `forward.args` and the arguments of `emit_osc`: a `{name}` placeholder becomes an `arg` or `capture` object (9.4).
- `midi_in`: the object of templates becomes the list of its types; a template other than the canonical one is `NotConvertible` (9.4).
- Closed vocabularies: `decode`, `expects`, `selects`, `sources[].type` and `sources[].license` take their terms from closed lists, mapped by the tables of section 6; a word outside the tables is `NotConvertible`.
- `capabilities`: derived from the fields present (`lua` when a field holds Lua), and absent when empty.
- Schemas: `$id` is the w3id address; the five files of `schemas/0.5/` are recorded in `schemas/SHA256SUMS`.

A converter MUST carry every change of a version when it converts from the version before.

A converter MUST NOT invent a value that a later version adds.

A converter MUST carry a descriptor forward only.

A converter MUST NOT carry a descriptor back to an earlier version.

> NOTE A published version never changes: 0.6 is frozen. A later 0.x version may still add and rename, never silently: each version converts from the one before, its converter carrying every change.

## 8 Frames and expressions (0.5)

### 8.1 Frames

This section describes the bytes of a message.

From 0.5, structure is never written in a string. A message (a frame) is a list of elements, at eight places: `commands[].frame`, `params.get_frame`, `params.set_frame`, `replies[].match_frame`, `identity.request`, `identity.reply`, `programs.before[]` and `programs.dump.request`. `sysex.header` and `sysex.footer` stay lists of bytes. An element is a byte (0 to 255) or an object of one key of its nature, with the keys that qualify it.

A reader MUST refuse an element that is an object:

- of two natures;
- of an unknown key;
- of a value outside the written lists.

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `{"arg": name}` | the command's argument `name`, one 7-bit byte |
| `{"arg": name, "encoding": e}` | `e` is `u14_msb`, `u14_lsb`, `s32`, `ascii` or `bytes`; the bytes are those said under this table |
| `{"arg": name, "add": n}` | the argument plus `n` (0 to 127), wrapping on 7 bits: `(v + n) & 0x7F` |
| `{"device_id": true}` | the device id: the rule `identity.device_id`, with its default value |
| `{"identity": "family"}`, `"member"` | all the bytes of the model; with `"index": n`, the byte `n` alone |
| `{"checksum": a, "from": n}` | `a` is `twos7` (Roland), `sum7` (Waldorf) or `xor7` (Fractal), on the bytes written from offset `n` (`F0` is 0), up to the checksum; an optional `"length": l` bounds the bytes summed |
| `"any"` | any byte, where a message is recognised |
| `{"capture": name}` | the byte read and kept under `name`, where a message is recognised |

**`u14_msb` and `u14_lsb`.** In a frame that is sent (`commands[].frame`, `params.get_frame`, `params.set_frame`) and for an unsigned value from 0 to 16383, each of the two writes one 7-bit byte, as MIDI cuts an unsigned value of 14 bits: `u14_msb` the bits 13 to 7 of the value, `u14_lsb` the bits 6 to 0, bit 0 being the least significant.

0.7 does not define:

- what `u14_msb` and `u14_lsb` write for an argument whose `range` goes below 0, for a value outside 0 to 16383, or in a frame where a message is recognised (`replies[].match_frame`);
- the bytes of `s32`, `ascii` and `bytes`;
- what the element `{"arg": name}`, without `encoding` or `add`, writes for a value below 0 or above 127;
- what the element `{"arg": "value"}`, without `encoding` or `add`, writes in `params.set_frame` under each `value_type` (`u7`, `u14`, `s32`).

A reader MUST refuse a byte outside 0 to 255.

A reader MUST refuse an `encoding`, a checksum algorithm, an `index` or an `add` outside the values written here.

A reader MUST refuse the word `any` outside `identity.reply` and `replies[].match_frame`.

A reader MUST refuse a capture where no message is recognised.

A reader MUST refuse an element written as a 0.4 string.

> NOTE The format has two additions. The `add` of a frame element wraps on 7 bits (8.1); the `add` of an expression clamps its result to 127 (8.2).

### 8.2 Expressions

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A `commands[].pre[].expr` is a tree: an integer from 0 to 127, `{"arg": name}`, or an object of one operation: `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `gt`, `lt`, `eq`, `ne` (each a list of two expressions) or `if` (a list of three: the condition, the value when it is not 0, the value otherwise).

The value is a byte of MIDI data, so every result is bounded to 0..127. The operations `gt`, `lt`, `eq` and `ne` give 0 or 1.

A reader MUST refuse a literal outside 0..127 when it reads the descriptor.

A reader MUST refuse a malformed tree, with its address.

A reader MUST refuse an expression written as a string.

A reader that evaluates an expression MUST clamp the result of `add`, `sub` and `mul` to 0..127.

A reader that evaluates an expression MUST NOT refuse the result of `add`, `sub` or `mul` for being out of range.

> NOTE This departs from JSON Logic, whose arithmetic is unbounded and whose `if` is the same list of three: the engine that runs these expressions (osc-bridge) writes MIDI data bytes, which 7 bits hold.

### 8.3 Conversion from 0.4

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A converter MUST turn each 0.4 string form into its structure.

A converter MUST refuse `{checksum}` without an algorithm and `{function}`, which have no structure, as `NotConvertible` at the address of the element.

## 9 Addresses, arguments and `midi_in` (0.5)

### 9.1 Addresses

This section describes the OSC addresses a tool uses and the MIDI messages they stand for.

An address that takes captures is written at four places: `commands[].osc`, `forward.path` (in `commands[]` and `subscriptions[]`), `replies[].match_osc` and `replies[].emit_osc`. It is either a string without brace (an address without capture) or an array of segments, each a string without brace or `{"capture": name}`.

A descriptor MUST write the `name` of a capture as `[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_]*`.

A reader MUST read an address as the concatenation of its segments.

A reader MUST NOT split an address on a slash.

A reader MUST refuse an array of segments that holds no segment, more than 16 segments, or no capture.

A reader MUST refuse a `commands[].osc` whose first segment is not a string starting with `/`.

A reader MUST refuse two captures side by side in an address that is recognised (`commands[].osc`, `replies[].match_osc`), as the first would take everything.

A reader MUST read two captures side by side in an address that is emitted (`forward.path`, `replies[].emit_osc`).

A reader MUST refuse a string with a brace as an address, at the address.

### 9.2 Arguments

This section describes the OSC arguments a tool uses.

The arguments of `forward.args` and `replies[].emit_args` are each a JSON literal (number, boolean, string without brace), `{"arg": name}` (the value of an argument of the command) or `{"capture": name}` (a field captured in the message recognised). `emit_osc` is the address alone (a string without space or brace, or segments); `emit_args` lists its arguments, and absent means none.

A reader MUST refuse an argument that is any other object, or an array, at the argument.

### 9.3 `midi_in`

This section describes the MIDI messages a device sends and the OSC addresses they are reported at.

`midi_in` is the list of the MIDI messages the device sends that are reported, each of `note_on`, `note_off`, `cc`, `pitchbend`, `aftertouch`, `poly_aftertouch`, `program_change`. Its point of view is the host's: the messages the device sends, seen from the host that receives them (`in` is the host's input). `[]` reports nothing.

A reader MUST refuse a `midi_in` that names a type twice.

A reader MUST refuse a `midi_in` of a type outside the seven, of a non-string, or of an object.

A reader MUST report each type at the address and with the arguments of this table, under the device's prefix; what follows the space are its OSC arguments, in this order.

| Type | Address and arguments |
|---|---|
| `note_on` | `/note/on {note} {velocity} {channel}` |
| `note_off` | `/note/off {note} {velocity} {channel}` |
| `cc` | `/cc/{num} {value} {channel}` |
| `pitchbend` | `/pitchbend {value_u14} {channel}` |
| `aftertouch` | `/aftertouch {value} {channel}` |
| `poly_aftertouch` | `/poly_aftertouch {note} {value} {channel}` |
| `program_change` | `/program_change {program} {channel}` |

> NOTE The words are those of 0.5, kept; the norm (MIDI 1.0 Detailed Specification) names three of them otherwise: `pitchbend` is Pitch Bend Change, `aftertouch` is Channel Pressure (one value for the whole channel) and `poly_aftertouch` is Polyphonic Key Pressure (one value per note).

### 9.4 Conversion from 0.4

This section describes the OSC addresses and the MIDI messages.

A converter MUST cut each 0.4 address that holds `{name}` into segments.

A converter MUST leave a 0.4 address without brace as it is.

A converter MUST write a `{name}` of a 0.4 `forward.args` as `{"arg": name}`.

A converter MUST cut a 0.4 `emit_osc` at its first space, the address before it and each `{name}` after it a `{"capture": name}` of `emit_args`.

A converter MUST refuse, as `NotConvertible` at the address of the element, naming the address or the template:

- a brace unclosed, empty, nested, stray or around anything but a name;
- an argument that mixes a brace with text;
- an argument of `emit_osc` that is not one `{name}`;
- a `midi_in` template other than the canonical one of its type.

## 10 What in a message is the sound (0.6)

### 10.1 Sound transfers

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A device states, in `sound_transfers[]`, what in a message is the sound. It describes the structure (which bytes make the sound); how a device codes it stays the device's. An entry is one form a sound travels in (alone, in a bank).

A device descriptor MAY state `sound_transfers`.

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | the name of the form; a reader is asked for the identity of a sound by it |
| `carries` | `sound` (one sound) or `bank` |
| `sound_id` | the same `sound_id` in two forms declares that their data is the same sound |
| `match_frame` | the message recognised, one element per byte (the message is as long as the list); the bytes that a transport part covers are not compared |
| `elements` | the elements of the transfer |
| `count`, `stride` | a bank only: the number of sounds, and the shift of each one: the sound `i` has its elements `i × stride` bytes further |

A descriptor MUST give both `count` and `stride` in a transfer that carries a bank.

A descriptor MUST NOT give `count` or `stride` in a transfer that carries a sound.

An element is `{from, length}` in the message (`F0` is 0) when its place is fixed, or `{script}`, a Lua script, when positions cannot say it. `data` is the sound itself; `name`, `channel`, `device_number`, `header`, `checksum`, `bank_number` and `program_number` are optional, the last six being the transport parts.

A descriptor MUST give `data` among the `elements` of a transfer.

A descriptor MUST give a name that lies inside the data as `name: {in_data: {from, length}}`, its place in the data unpacked, counted in bytes from 0 (the first byte of the unpacked data), whatever the packing.

### 10.2 The coding of the data

This section describes the bytes of a message.

`data` adds `coding`, a closed list: `none` (default), the bytes of the message are the data; `msb_first_7in8`, groups of 8 bytes, a first byte that carries the bits 7 of the seven that follow (its bit k is the bit 7 of the byte k) then those seven bytes with their bits 6 to 0, the last group possibly partial.

A descriptor MAY give any other coding by a script.

The word for the 7-bit coding is `coding`, at two places new in 0.6: `data.coding` and `coding` of the `write` step of a file write (11.2). `identity.unpack` (published in 0.5) is the third place, for the reply of the identify request; it keeps its spelling, `lsb_8to7`.

The norm's own coding, Mcoded7 (M2-103-UM v1.2, 6.1.7), is `coding: mcoded7` of a step: the sign bits of a group of seven bytes are sent first, the first byte's sign at bit 6 (0ABCDEFG), then the seven low parts (0AAAaaaa …); a short last group is coded the same way (3 bytes, 0ABC0000 then three bytes).

A reader MUST code bytes in 7 bits with `mcoded7` and with `msb_first_7in8` as this subsection defines them, giving the coded bytes that the cases of `conformance/0.6/coding/` give.

> NOTE The piece of `msb_first_7in8` is the KORG minilogue xd MIDI Implementation (revision 1.01, 2020.2.10), note 1 "7 bit data format conversion"; the NTS-1 note gives a partial last group (11 bytes of MIDI for 9 of data). The cases of `conformance/0.6/coding/` show the bytes, built on the norm's two examples of Mcoded7 (a full group of seven bytes, a short group of three).

### 10.3 The identity of a sound

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A reader that computes the identity of a sound MUST compute the SHA-256 of its data element alone, in 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits, the data being unpacked and the unpacked bytes of a name that lies inside the data (`in_data`) being set to zero, the length kept.

A reader that computes the identity of a sound MUST NOT let the name, the device number, the channel, the header, the checksum, the bank number or the program number enter it.

A reader without Lua MUST determine at load, for each transfer, whether it computes the identity.

A reader without Lua MUST NOT compute the identity of a sound whose `data` is a script.

A reader without Lua MUST compute the identity of a sound when only a transport part or the name holds a script, a name given by a script being taken as outside the data.

> NOTE The same sound renamed, on another channel or in a bank has one identity. This follows KnobKraft Orm, which keeps a patch's fingerprint to its data and masks the channel, and departs from it by its hash (it hashes with MD5): SHA-256 is a named hash with no known collisions, the same on every tool; the identity another tool gives (the MD5 of an adaptation) is carried beside it (section 13), not in its place.

### 10.4 What a reader refuses

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault:

- an element outside the message (the name included, the last sound of a bank included);
- an empty data;
- a data that overlaps the header, the checksum or a transport part;
- two transport parts that overlap, at the later, in the order of the table of 10.1;
- an element given by positions and by a script;
- a name given by positions of the message that overlaps the data;
- a name `in_data` that goes past the unpacked data or lies on a data given by a script;
- a name that mixes `in_data` with `from`, `length` or `script`;
- a `coding` word outside the list;
- a script without the `lua` capability;
- the `lua` capability without a script.

## 11 What a device can receive (0.6)

### 11.1 Resources

This section describes the files a device receives, at the level of the musician and of the bytes.

A device says, in `resources[]`, what it can receive from another, so that placing a description on a target asks for no code of the target. The words are those of the ResourceList of MIDI 2.0 Property Exchange: `resource`, `canSet` (`none`, `partial`, `full`) and `mediaTypes`. OSD adds two fields: `export`, the name of the format that produces the content, and `via`, the way the content arrives by on a device that does not speak Property Exchange (absent, Property Exchange sets the resource). The only way is `file_write`: a `path`, a list of segments (a fixed text, or `{"variable": "vendor"}` or `{"variable": "device"}`, with an optional `suffix` for a text that follows the value), and `carried_by` (`sysex`).

A device descriptor MAY declare `resources`.

A descriptor MUST give a `resource` as any non-empty name: one of the list of Property Exchange, or a name of its own.

A reader MUST refuse an empty `resource` name.

A reader MUST refuse a `canSet` outside `none`, `partial` and `full`.

A descriptor MUST give `export` as a non-empty text.

A reader MUST read as it stands an `export` it does not know.

A reader MUST NOT refuse an `export` for being unknown.

A reader MUST refuse a way other than `file_write`, at its key.

A reader MUST refuse a `path` given as a template string.

A reader MUST refuse a `path` with no segment.

A reader MUST refuse a `carried_by` other than `sysex`, at the field.

A reader MUST fill the variables of a `path` from the `vendor` and the `name` of the identity of the device to place.

A reader MUST refuse a value that is empty or holds `/` or `\`, naming the segment of the path and the field of the device.

> EXAMPLE The Deluge (community firmware 1.3) reads `MIDI_DEVICES/DEFINITION/<vendor>/<device>.xml` on its card. The Minilogue (Korg, minilogue) gives `MIDI_DEVICES/DEFINITION/Korg/minilogue.xml`. A new target is added by its description alone, with no code: its `resources` says the format it takes and where.
>
> ```json
> "resources": [{
>   "resource": "X-DeviceDefinition",
>   "canSet": "full",
>   "mediaTypes": ["application/xml"],
>   "export": "deluge",
>   "via": { "file_write": {
>     "path": ["MIDI_DEVICES", "DEFINITION", {"variable": "vendor"}, {"variable": "device", "suffix": ".xml"}],
>     "carried_by": "sysex"
>   } }
> }]
> ```

> NOTE Property Exchange writes the prefix `X-` for a name of its own; OSD does not require it. The format names no tool. The consent before a write on a device is the rule of the tool that writes, not a field.

### 11.2 How the file is written by MIDI

This section describes the bytes of a message.

A descriptor MAY add to a `file_write` `steps`, the messages by which the file is written, with no script. One `open`, one `write` and one `close` (the write is played once per chunk, in order, to the end of the file). Each step says:

- `step`: `open`, `write` or `close`;
- `send`: the message, a list of the elements of a frame (a byte, `device_id`, `identity`…) plus three of the file write: `{"text": "…"}` (printable ASCII, written as its bytes), `{"file": "path" | "size" | "offset" | "chunk_size" | "chunk"}` and `{"captured": "<name>"}`; `"as": "decimal"` writes a number as its ASCII digits, and a number without `as` (`size`, `offset`, `chunk_size`) is written as 4 bytes of 7 bits, the most significant first (a value of 28 bits at most), the form a SysEx message takes for a number (see the rows `file.size` and `file.offset` of 21.1);
- `reply`, when a reply is awaited: the recognised elements of a frame (`any`, `capture`…) plus `{"capture_json": "<key>"}`, which keeps the value of that key of a JSON text reply under that name (a name, no expression); `reply_timeout_ms` goes with it;
- `delay_ms`: the pause before the next step.

The `write` step also says `chunk_bytes` (1 or more) and `coding`: `mcoded7` (Mcoded7, see 10.2), or `msb_first_7in8`, the coding `sound_transfers` already names (a coding is named by its bit order, never by a device).

A descriptor MUST make a `captured` element name a `capture_json` of the reply of the `open` step.

A descriptor MUST NOT write the variable parts of a message as a template in a string: they are objects the schema checks.

A reader MUST accept a `file_write` that gives only its `path`.

A reader MUST refuse, at their place:

- a step outside the three;
- a `chunk_bytes` of zero;
- a reply without `reply_timeout_ms`;
- a `reply_timeout_ms` shorter than the `delay_ms` of the step;
- a missing or second `open`, `write` or `close`;
- a `coding` outside the list.

> EXAMPLE The Deluge opens the file by a JSON command and keeps the file id of its reply, writes at most 1024 bytes per message, then closes it. The messages are built from the code of the community firmware (DelugeFirmware at 680b93c), not observed on a device; the 1000 ms wait for a reply is a choice of OSD, the firmware gives none.
>
> ```json
> "via": { "file_write": {
>   "path": ["MIDI_DEVICES", "DEFINITION", {"variable": "vendor"}, {"variable": "device", "suffix": ".xml"}],
>   "carried_by": "sysex",
>   "steps": [
>     { "step": "open",
>       "send": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 4, 0, {"text": "{\"open\":{\"path\":\""}, {"file": "path"}, {"text": "\",\"write\":1}}"}, 247],
>       "reply": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 5, "any", {"capture_json": "fid"}],
>       "reply_timeout_ms": 1000 },
>     { "step": "write", "chunk_bytes": 1024, "coding": "msb_first_7in8",
>       "send": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 4, 0, {"text": "{\"write\":{\"fid\":"}, {"captured": "fid", "as": "decimal"},
>                {"text": ",\"addr\":"}, {"file": "offset", "as": "decimal"}, {"text": ",\"size\":"},
>                {"file": "chunk_size", "as": "decimal"}, {"text": "}}"}, 0, {"file": "chunk"}, 247],
>       "reply": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 5, "any"], "reply_timeout_ms": 1000 },
>     { "step": "close",
>       "send": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 4, 0, {"text": "{\"close\":{\"fid\":"}, {"captured": "fid", "as": "decimal"}, {"text": "}}"}, 247],
>       "reply": [240, 0, 33, 123, 1, 5, "any"], "reply_timeout_ms": 1000 }
>   ]
> } }
> ```

## 12 A sound read setting by setting (0.6)

### 12.1 Places

This section describes both levels: the settings a musician reads and the bytes where they lie.

A sound transfer adds `places`, a list that says, for each control, where its value lies in the data of the sound. The place is written on the transfer, because it depends on the form the data takes; a control carries no place. The word is `places`, not `settings`: `settings` of a sound (`content.settings`, published in 0.5) are the values of a sound by address of control, another thing. Each place is:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `control` | the JSON pointer of the control it sets: `/cc_params/entries/N` or `/params/entries/N`, an entry the description has |
| `from`, `length` | whole bytes of the data, unpacked, the name in place (`from` 0 is its first byte); the value is the unsigned integer of those bytes, the first the highest (two bytes, high then low, are `length: 2`) |
| `script` | a Lua script that gives the value from the data, for a device no list can describe; the place carries no `from` and no `length` |

A reader MUST refuse a `control` pointer outside this pattern or naming an absent entry, at the `control`, before any other fault of the setting.

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault:

- a place that goes past the data (unpacked, the name included);
- a place and a script together;
- a script without the `lua` capability;
- the `lua` capability without a script;
- `settings` on a 0.6 transfer, as an unknown field.

**A control placed twice (0.7).** Two places of one transfer that name the same control differ when their `from`, their `length` or their `script` differ. Such places contradict each other and nothing in the transfer says which one holds: both are to be confirmed. In 0.7 only another transfer is another context.

A reader MUST accept two places of one transfer that name the same control, and keep them as written, in their order.

A reader MUST leave a control that a transfer places at places that differ out of the settings of a sound of that transfer, and so out of a comparison of two such sounds.

A reader MUST warn of each place of such a control, as `UnconfirmedPlace` at its `control` (`/sound_transfers/N/places/M/control`), in the order of the transfers, then of the places.

A reader MUST read identical places of one transfer as one place, with no warning.

A reader MUST read the places of two transfers each in its own transfer.

A converter MUST carry every place to 0.7, unchanged and in its order.

Cases: `0.7/read/control-placed-twice-in-a-transfer-accepted`, `0.7/convert/control-placed-twice-in-a-transfer-carried`

### 12.2 Settings and comparison

This section describes the settings a musician reads.

A reader MUST give, for a sound of a message, the list of its settings: the control, its role (the `paramPath` that `roles` gives) and its raw value, in the order of the controls (`cc_params`, then `params`).

A reader MUST leave a control out of the settings when it has no role, when it has no place, when it is read by a script (which the reader does not play), or when the data does not hold its place.

A reader MUST NOT guess a place or a value.

A reader MUST give a sound of a bank and a single sound of the same data the same settings.

A reader MUST compare the settings of two sounds of one device by role, and give for each `equal`, `differ` or `absent`.

A reader MUST give `absent`, and never `equal`, for a setting that one of the two sounds lacks.

### 12.3 A real device: the Korg minilogue xd (informative)

The piece is the KORG minilogue xd MIDI Implementation (public document of the maker), TABLE 2 "PROGRAM PARAMETER", which gives each parameter an offset in the program data, a bit range and a value range. Three lines are taken as they stand:

| Offset | Bit | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | | 0~4 | OCTAVE (0~4 = -2~+2) |
| 24, 25 | H:0~7, L:0~7 | 0~1023 | VCO 1 PITCH (note P5) |
| 162 | 0 | 0,1 | Step 1 Active Step Off/On |

According to the document, not read on a device, the place of each is: OCTAVE one whole byte (`from: 16, length: 1`); VCO 1 PITCH the byte 24 marked H then the byte 25 marked L, each with its bits 0 to 7 (`from: 24, length: 2`, the first the highest), the document saying nothing more of how the range 0~1023 lies in those 16 bits, so the raw value is the integer of the two bytes and no more; Step 1 Active one bit of the byte 162 (bit 0). Offsets are taken as places in the data as the table gives them. The first two are written on the sound transfer:

```json
"places": [
  { "control": "/params/entries/0", "from": 16, "length": 1 },
  { "control": "/params/entries/1", "from": 24, "length": 2 }
]
```

(`/params/entries/0` and `/1` being the controls OCTAVE and VCO 1 PITCH of the descriptor.) Two sounds whose byte 16 is `02` and `03`, the bytes 24 and 25 being the same, compare as: OCTAVE `differ` (2 against 3), VCO 1 PITCH `equal`. Step 1 Active is a bit of a byte: OSD 0.7 has no place by bits, so it is not placed: it is a setting of neither sound, and the comparison does not list it (`absent` is for a setting that one of the two sounds has and the other lacks, 12.2).

## 13 The identities other tools give a sound (0.6)

This section describes the identities that other tools give a sound, at the level of the musician.

A 0.6 sound descriptor MAY hold `external_ids`, a list of the identities other tools give the same sound, so that a tool which holds it recognises it. Each entry is `{tool, by, algorithm, value}`: `tool` (required) names the tool, such as `KnobKraft Orm`; `by` names what in the tool computes the identity per device, such as the name of a KnobKraft adaptation; `algorithm` names the algorithm when it is known, such as `md5`; `value` (required) is the value as the tool writes it. The field is allowed for every `kind`. The list never replaces the identity of a sound (10.3): the identity of OSD is the SHA-256 of its data.

> NOTE The model is the external identifiers of Wikidata (an item keeps the identifier each other catalogue gives it, with that catalogue's name). For KnobKraft Orm, the piece is the guide of KnobKraft Orm (`docs/programming-guide.md`, revision 735e96c9), on `calculateFingerprint` (lines 872 and 924): each adaptation writes its own `calculateFingerprint`, which hashes the bytes it chooses with MD5 and returns 32 hexadecimal digits. The list does not promise that two tools agree on what the data of a sound is: each value says only what that tool gives. The md5 and the sha1 of one adaptation are not the same computation.

A descriptor MUST NOT write the identity of OSD in `external_ids`.

A reader MUST refuse the tool `OSD`, in any case.

A reader MUST refuse a value outside the form of a known algorithm, read without case: `md5` 32, `sha1` 40, `sha256` 64 lower-case hexadecimal digits.

A reader MUST accept any non-empty value for an unknown or absent algorithm.

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault:

- an entry without `tool` or `value`, as `Missing`;
- an unknown key of an entry, as `UnknownField`;
- an empty value;
- two entries of the same computation (same `tool`, `by` and `algorithm`, each compared without case, an absent one equal to an absent one) with different values, at the later one, as `BadValue`.

## 14 The devices and base versions a firmware is made for (0.6)

This section describes the firmwares a device runs, at the level of the musician.

A firmware descriptor of 0.6 (of any `kind`: `firmware`, `mod`, `module`, `application`, `script`, `extension`) names `targets`, a non-empty list. One module that runs on several devices and several base versions says so once, a target per device.

A descriptor of kind firmware MUST name `targets`.

A reader MUST refuse a `device`, a `based_on` or a `midi` at the first level of a 0.6 firmware, as `UnknownField`; each is written in a target.

A target is `{device, midi, based_on, runs_on}`. `device` is `{vendor, model}` with the optional `descriptor` (`maker/file`), required. `midi` is optional and is what the firmware's `midi` was: `{descriptor, same_as, changes}`, here for this device alone. `based_on` is optional and is the lineage only: `{name, version, url}`, `name` required, `version` one text, the firmware it was forked or built from; it says nothing of what the target runs on. `runs_on` is optional: `{name, versions}`, `name` required, no `url`; `versions` is written in one of two forms:

- a list: the versions the target runs on, one or more;
- a range `{min, max}`: its bounds are included; a `min` alone is open upward, a `max` alone open downward; at least one of the two.

> EXAMPLE
>
> ```json
> "targets": [
>   {"device": {"vendor": "Elektron", "model": "Digitone"},
>    "midi": {"descriptor": "elektron/digitone-midi"},
>    "based_on": {"name": "Elektron OS", "version": "1.42"},
>    "runs_on": {"name": "Elektron OS", "versions": ["1.43", "1.44"]}},
>   {"device": {"vendor": "Elektron", "model": "Digitone Keys"},
>    "midi": {"same_as": "elektron/digitone-midi", "changes": "the keys"},
>    "runs_on": {"name": "Elektron OS", "versions": {"min": "1.40B", "max": "1.41"}}}
> ]
> ```

A reader MUST refuse a `based_on` that holds `versions`, as `UnknownField`.

A descriptor MUST write a compared version (a `runs_on` version, a range bound, a sound's `min_version`) as `$defs/version`: components separated by one dot, each a number then optional letters.

> NOTE `1.40C`, `2.0` and `10` are admitted; `1..2`, `B`, `.1`, `1.` and `0.3-beta` are refused.

A reader MUST refuse, at the element, as `Missing` or `BadValue`: a target without `device`, a range without `min` nor `max`, an empty list of versions, and a compared version outside that grammar.

A reader MUST NOT refuse the `version` of a firmware, or the `version` of its `based_on`, for its form: it is free text, only displayed.

A reader MUST compare two versions component by component: the text splits on `.`; each component is a number followed by optional letters; and

- numbers compare as numbers, then the letters without case, no letters before any letters;
- a missing component is 0 without letters.

A reader MUST test a list of versions by the same rule.

> NOTE So 1.40 < 1.40B < 1.40C < 1.41, 1.9 < 1.10 and 1.40 = 1.40.0; a list is tested by the same rule (1.40.0 fits `["1.40"]`). This is not SemVer: base firmware numbers such as 1.40C do not follow it. The model is Debian's version comparison, a written algorithm for numbers that are not SemVer, with two departures and their reason: letters are compared without case, because a maker writes 1.40b and 1.40B for one version; and 1.40 = 1.40.0, because a trailing zero changes no firmware version.

A reader MUST answer unknown when it compares a text outside the grammar (the `version` of a firmware, read free).

A reader MUST NOT invent an order for a text outside the grammar.

A reader MUST answer yes, no or unknown to the question whether a version fits a target.

A reader MUST answer unknown for a target without `runs_on` versions (no `runs_on`, or a `runs_on` that gives only its `name`), whatever its `based_on` says.

A reader MUST NOT answer yes or no for a target without `runs_on` versions.

> NOTE A lineage is not a constraint. So the `min_version` "1.2" of a sound against the module version "0.3-beta" is unknown. The caller decides what to do with an unknown; OSD makes it neither a refusal nor an acceptance.

A converter MUST write a 0.5 firmware (a 0.4 one passes through 0.5) with one `device`, one `midi` and one `based_on` as one target holding the three as they are, the `based_on` staying the lineage `{name, version, url}`.

A converter MUST NOT write a `runs_on` for a converted target.

A converter MUST return a 0.6 descriptor as it is.

> NOTE What a converted descriptor loses is the constraint it never had: its "based on 1.2" stays the lineage and is not turned into "runs only on 1.2", and the target answers unknown to every version.

Cases: `0.6/firmware-targets-list`, `0.6/firmware-targets-range`, `0.6/firmware-targets-several-devices`, `0.6/firmware-targets-compare-versions` (it states yes, no and unknown, equal, lower, higher and letters), `0.6/firmware-version-outside-grammar-unknown`, `0.6/firmware-target-lineage-apart-from-runs-on`, `0.6/firmware-midi-at-first-level-refused`, `0.6/firmware-based-on-versions-refused`, `0.6/firmware-target-without-device-refused`, and the conversions `0.6/firmware-device-becomes-target`, `0.6/firmware-0-4-device-becomes-target`, `0.6/firmware-midi-moves-into-the-target`.

## 15 The firmware a builder made: the assembly (0.6)

This section describes the firmware a person runs, at the level of the musician.

An assembly descriptor (`schemas/0.6/assembly.schema.json`, from 0.6 only) says which firmware a person runs: a stock firmware and the modules applied to it, each at its exact version. The builder of that firmware writes it, for the firmware it built.

The kinds of firmware that an assembly uses: `firmware` is a stock firmware, the one a device ships with or a vendor releases; `mod` is a whole modified firmware, a firmware that is itself the result of changing a stock one and that is installed in its place; `module` is a plug-in applied to a stock firmware, one among several that an assembly stacks on it. A program installed on a host system (section 19) is of kind `application`.

An assembly descriptor MAY name as its `stock` a firmware of kind `application`.

| Field | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `$schema` | the 0.6 assembly address | required |
| `stock` | `{firmware, version}`, required | the stock firmware: the catalogue id of its descriptor (kind `firmware`) and its exact version |
| `modules` | list of `{module, version}`, required, an empty list is valid | the modules: the catalogue id of each descriptor (kind `module`) and the exact version applied, in the order applied |

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault, by the reader and by the schema:

- an unknown field, as `UnknownField`;
- an absent `stock` or `modules`, and a `stock` or a module without `version` (or `firmware`, `module`), as `Missing`;
- a `stock` that is text, and a list of modules that is not a list of objects, as `BadValue`.

> EXAMPLE
>
> ```json
> {"$schema": "https://w3id.org/osd/0.6/assembly.schema.json",
>  "stock": {"firmware": "acme/stock", "version": "1.0"},
>  "modules": [{"module": "acme/mod-a", "version": "1.3"}, {"module": "acme/mod-b", "version": "2.0"}]}
> ```
>
> With the stock MIDI `acme/synth` (routes `/p1`, `/p2`), `mod-a` whose MIDI descriptor adds `/p3` and `mod-b` whose adds `/p4`, the assembly resolves to the routes `/p1`, `/p2`, `/p3`, `/p4`.

> NOTE An assembly is written as a lockfile records the exact combination a tool built (each entry of Cargo.lock is a name and an exact version). OSD does not build an assembly, does not verify that one is a sound combination and does not choose one: it reads what the builder wrote. The resolution of an assembly is in 23.37 to 23.41.

Cases: `0.6/assembly`, `0.6/assembly-stock-absent-refused`, `0.6/assembly-unknown-field-refused`, `0.6/assembly-modules-malformed-refused`, `0.6/assembly-stock-version-absent-refused`, `0.6/assembly-stock-text-refused`, `0.6/assembly-module-version-absent-refused`.

## 16 The modules a sound needs and the assembly it was made on (0.6)

This section describes the modules a sound uses, at the level of the musician.

A 0.6 sound descriptor says three things about firmware, each optional and none overriding another:

| Field | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `firmware` | text | the firmware it was made on, in words, for people |
| `needs` | list of `{module, min_version?}` | the need: the modules the sound uses, which a reader judges |
| `made_on` | an assembly without its `$schema`, defined once in `schemas/0.6/assembly.schema.json` | the provenance: the exact assembly of its author, read as written |

`module` is the catalogue id of a module's firmware descriptor (kind `module`); `min_version` is the least version of that module the sound needs. A sound without `needs` needs no module. `made_on.stock` is the stock firmware of the sound's `device`: the firmware that device ships with, on which the modules of `made_on.modules` were applied.

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault, by the reader and by the schema:

- an empty `module` or `min_version`, as `BadValue`;
- an entry without `module`, as `Missing`;
- an unknown key of an entry, as `UnknownField`;
- a `$schema` inside `made_on`.

A reader MUST read `made_on` as the form of the assembly.

A reader MUST NOT refuse a sound whose `device` and `made_on.stock` disagree, or whose `firmware`, `needs` and `made_on` disagree.

A resolving reader MUST give, for each entry of `needs` in order, a verdict, reading nothing but the sound and the assembly.

A resolving reader MUST judge a need on the version the assembly pins for the module (`modules[].version`), never on a catalogue descriptor.

A resolving reader MUST give the verdict:

- `satisfied` when the `module` of the need figures in the `modules` of the assembly and, if `min_version` is written, when the version pinned there is not lower by the comparison of versions of section 14;
- `not_satisfied` when the module is absent from the assembly, or when the pinned version is lower than `min_version`;
- `unknown` when the pinned version, or `min_version`, is one the comparison of versions cannot read (`nightly`): neither satisfied nor refused.

An entry without `min_version` asks only that the module is present, at whatever version. A sound is playable on an assembly when no need is not satisfied and none unknown.

A resolving reader MUST NOT let `made_on` enter the judgement of needs.

> NOTE `requires` and `needs` are two distinct fields: `requires` is what a device's programs need first, `needs` the modules a sound uses. A tool may show the unknown verdicts. A sound made with mod-a 1.0 on a catalogue now at 1.3 is judged on the version of the assembly asked about. No mature reference puts this judgement in a format's reader: Cargo judges requirements in its resolver, a tool, and toml-test and the WebAssembly specification judge only reading. OSD departs from it, by the supervisor's decision: its reader already resolves an assembly's MIDI, and a rule that two readers must apply alike is proven only by a case a reader runs. The conformance cases of the judgement (`conformance/0.6/judge_needs/<case>/`: `sound.json`, `assembly.json`, `case.json` with `judge_needs: true` and a `result` of `{module, verdict}`) are run by every resolving reader.

Cases: `0.6/sound-needs`, `0.6/sound-made-on-assembly`, `0.6/sound-needs-and-made-on`, `0.6/sound-needs-module-empty-refused`, `0.6/sound-needs-min-version-empty-refused`, `0.6/sound-modules-min-version-outside-grammar-refused` (reading); `0.6/judge_needs/need-met-by-pinned-version`, `need-not-met-by-pinned-version`, `need-without-min-version-module-present`, `need-module-absent`, `need-pinned-version-unreadable` (judgement).

## 17 The builder of a module (0.6)

This section describes the tool that builds a firmware, at the level of the musician.

A builder (Elekloader, Modwerk) is the tool that makes an assembly from the stock OS and modules; the person flashes the result. The `install.method` can be `builder` (one of the examples of the method), and `steps` and `revert` stay free text, `revert` saying how to go back to stock.

A firmware descriptor of 0.6, of any `kind`, MAY name a `builder`, an object with a `name` and a `url` (its address).

A descriptor MUST give both `name` and `url` in a `builder`.

A descriptor whose `install.method` is `builder` MUST name a `builder`.

A reader MUST refuse any other key of a `builder`.

A reader MUST accept a `builder` beside another install method (`usb-drive`).

> NOTE The builder stays the reference for its modules: it is where a reader is sent to build, install and update, and OSD never rehosts the assembly or the modules.

Cases: `0.6/firmware-builder`, `0.6/firmware-builder-without-url-refused`.

## 18 Pauses, reply time and banks that cannot be written (0.6)

This section describes the timing of a device, at the level of the bytes of a message.

`programs.delay_ms` (0.5) is the one pause, in milliseconds, between the messages of a recall.

`programs.dump.reply_timeout_ms` (0.6, integer from 0) is how long the device can take to answer the request of the dump, in milliseconds.

A reader MUST refuse a `programs.dump.reply_timeout_ms` shorter than `programs.delay_ms`, as `BadValue` at `/programs/dump/reply_timeout_ms`.

A reader MUST count a missing pause as zero.

`programs.banks[].writable` (0.6, boolean) says whether the bank can be written.

A reader MUST read a bank that gives no `writable` as writable.

> NOTE A device that needs different pauses after different messages takes the longest: that need (the Yamaha TG33 in KnobKraft Orm's `messageSendDelays`) is known and waits for a second device before the format carries a pause per message or a sequence of steps. `reply_timeout_ms` is KnobKraft Orm's `replyTimeoutMs`; the schema cannot compare two fields, the reader does, and the rule is a rule of meaning of OSD, not a fact KnobKraft Orm states. `writable: false` is KnobKraft Orm's `isROM`, with the meaning turned to the positive word. A tool never offers to write into a bank that is not writable. OSD describes no sequence that stores a sound yet, so no reader refuses a bank as the target of one.

## 19 A program, and the devices it runs on (0.6)

This section describes the programs and the devices they run on, at the level of the musician.

Two ways name a program, and the choice is made by the link to a device. A program installed on a host system of a device, alike on several devices, is a firmware descriptor of kind `application` (a program installed on the host system of a device) with `targets`: one target per device, each with its `runs_on` (the host system), its `midi` written once and reused by `same_as` in the other targets, and each device has its own descriptor, whose `identity.usb` recognises it. A program that no firmware target ties to a device, whatever the host machine, is a device descriptor with `device.kind: software`.

`application` is the word of `kind` that 0.6 adds. A reader MUST refuse the kind `application` in a firmware of 0.4 or 0.5, as `BadValue` at `/kind`, as it refuses `module`.

A reader MUST accept a device descriptor that has an identity and no MIDI of its own (no `params`, `controls` or `commands`).

> NOTE Such a device is a host for programs, recognised by its USB identity.

Cases: `0.6/firmware-application-on-two-devices`, `0.6/device-identity-without-midi-first`, `0.6/device-identity-without-midi-second`, `0.6/device-kind-software-host-indifferent` (reading); `0.6/assembly-program-on-first-device`, `0.6/assembly-program-on-second-device` (resolution).

## 20 A virtual instrument and its fidelity (0.6)

This section describes the virtual instruments, at the level of the musician.

A virtual instrument is an entry like another: a software device (`device.kind` `software`) that stands for a device of the catalogue says so, and says how true it is to it. Two optional fields of `device`:

- `stands_for` is the catalogue address `maker/device` of the device it stands for;
- `fidelity` is one of three words, each a claim about the method, not a grade:
  - `faithful`: the real firmware, emulated;
  - `modelled`: the behaviour of the device reproduced from study, without its firmware;
  - `principle`: a design that follows the device's principle without claiming its behaviour.

A descriptor MAY name as the `stands_for` target a software device.

A descriptor MAY give `stands_for` without `fidelity`.

A reader MUST refuse `fidelity` without `stands_for`, at `/device/fidelity`.

A reader MUST refuse `stands_for` and `fidelity` on a hardware device, or on a device that gives no `kind`, at the field.

A reader MUST refuse a `stands_for` that is not of the form `maker/device`, and a `fidelity` outside the three words, as `BadValue` at the field.

A reader MUST check the form of `stands_for` alone when it reads a descriptor.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a device whose `stands_for` names an address absent from the catalogue, as `BadValue` at `/device/stands_for` (23.36).

A resolving reader MUST resolve a software device that stands for nothing without looking for a target.

A reader MUST accept a software device that stands for no other device, a plugin or a DAW of its own, with neither field.

> NOTE MAME states for each driver how well the emulation works (good, imperfect, preliminary), a grade of the result. OSD departs from it: the fidelity states how true a virtual instrument is by its method, a fact its maker knows and a reader can check against the sources, where a grade of the result is a judgement that changes with each listener. The departure keeps the vocabulary of the claim honest, and the descriptor still names the device it shows, as a Hugging Face Space names the model it demonstrates.

## 21 The words 0.6 freezes, each defined (0.6)

### 21.1 The words defined

This section describes the words that had no written definition, at both levels.

A word published in 0.5 keeps its spelling and is defined here; a word new in 0.6 is named as the norm names the thing.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `param_entry.pr` | an entry of `params` (a parameter read and written by SysEx through one shared frame): the first of the four numbers (`pr`, `p`, `c`, `r`) that address the parameter in the maker's frame, as the maker's table gives them; one byte, 0 to 255; in 0.7 the field `pr` of its `sysex` way (5.3.3) |
| `param_entry.p` | the second of the four numbers (`pr`, `p`, `c`, `r`) that address the parameter in the maker's frame; one byte, 0 to 255; in 0.7 the field `p` of its `sysex` way (5.3.3) |
| `param_entry.c` | the third of the four numbers (`pr`, `p`, `c`, `r`) that address the parameter in the maker's frame; one byte, 0 to 255; in 0.7 the field `c` of its `sysex` way (5.3.3) |
| `param_entry.r` | the fourth of the four numbers (`pr`, `p`, `c`, `r`) that address the parameter in the maker's frame; one byte, 0 to 255; in 0.7 the field `r` of its `sysex` way (5.3.3) |
| `param_entry.id` | the parameter's own number when the maker numbers its parameters one by one: one integer, not limited to a byte; an entry gives the numbers its maker's frame uses and leaves the others out; in 0.7 the field `id` of its `sysex` way (5.3.3), where it may be negative as in 0.6 |
| `dump.kind` | how the current sound is taken from the device: `none` (it cannot be), `request` (the device sends it when the `request` message, or its `script`, is sent), `edit_buffer` (it sends the sound it is playing, with no request written here) or `manual` (only when the person starts the dump on the device) |
| `channel_mode` | of a trigger: how the device takes the channel of the note that fires it: `fixed` (the channel given only), `basic`, `omni` (any channel) or `auto` (the device chooses) |
| `polarity` | of a control (0.7; `orientation` in 0.6): where its values start: `unipolar` (the lowest value is the start, as a level) or `bipolar` (the middle value is the rest position, as a pan) |
| `custom_commands.profile` | osc-bridge extension: the behaviour of the bridge a custom command takes: `default`, or `preset_ingest` for a command that loads a whole preset |
| `file.size` | `{"file": "size"}` in a message of a file write: the size of the whole file in bytes; without `as`, four bytes of 7 bits, the most significant first (28 bits at most); with `"as": "decimal"`, its ASCII digits |
| `file.offset` | `{"file": "offset"}` in a message of a file write: the offset in the file of the chunk this `write` sends, in bytes; written as `file.size` is, four bytes of 7 bits without `as`, ASCII digits with it |
| `capabilities.lua` | the capability a descriptor declares when a field holds a Lua script or expression: version, Lua 5.4; inputs, what the site hands the script (`reply` for an identity script, `ctx` for a recall or dump script, `data` for a place, `message` for an element of a transfer); return, what the site expects (a boolean for an identity script, a list of lists of bytes for a recall, one list of bytes for a dump request or a custom command, an integer for a place, the bytes of the element for a transfer); a reader without Lua does not run it |

A descriptor MUST declare the `lua` capability when it holds Lua.

A reader MUST refuse a `capabilities` list that differs from the list that the fields of the descriptor require.

### 21.2 The hex text and the content of a sound

This section describes the bytes of a message and the content of a sound.

A descriptor MUST write a hex text in upper-case hexadecimal (`F0 42 3C`).

A reader MUST refuse a lower-case digit in a SysEx text, at the field.

A reader MUST refuse, at `/content`, a sound whose content holds none of `sysex`, `midi` and `settings`.

A converter MUST write a lower-case SysEx text of 0.5 as the same bytes in upper case.

A converter MUST refuse a sound without content, as `NotConvertible` at `/content`.

> NOTE The identity of a sound and the other digests keep their own form (64 lower-case digits for a SHA-256, as the tools write them). The conversion cases of these writings are `0.6/hex-lowercase-becomes-uppercase`, `0.6/control-mixing-cc-and-nrpn-refused` and `0.6/sound-without-content-refused`; the refusal of a control that mixes kinds is that of the conversion to 0.6 (21.3).

### 21.3 One message kind per control

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

In 0.6 a control (`cc_params` entry, and each way of `also`) names one message kind: a CC (with `cc_lsb` for 14 bits), an NRPN or an RPN, and the second way of reaching the control goes in `also`; in 0.7 each way of `ways` names one kind of message (5.3.3). A control of a fingerprint (`controls`, and `names[].control`) keeps its flat words in 0.7, without ways: `cc`, `nrpn_msb`, `nrpn_lsb` and `channel` from 0.5, and `cc_lsb`, `rpn_msb` and `rpn_lsb` too from 0.6; a reader refuses, as `UnknownField`, a word that the version of the text does not define.

For a text of 0.6, a reader MUST refuse the flat fields of an entry that mix two kinds, at the entry.

A reader MUST refuse a fingerprint control that gives more than one kind, at the control.

In 0.7 a control reaches each of its messages by a way (proposal 0009, 5.3.3), so a control of `cc_params` of 0.4 or 0.5 whose flat fields mix two kinds of message or three (a CC, an NRPN, an RPN) has a form in 0.7 that 0.6 does not give it. The rules below are those of its conversion.

A converter that converts a text of 0.4 or 0.5 to 0.7 MUST make every change and every refusal of the conversion to 0.6, then every change and every refusal of the conversion from 0.6 to 0.7, but one: it does not refuse a control of `cc_params` for mixing kinds of message. The rest of the file, the other entries and the other fields, converts as in the same file without that control, its refusals included.

A converter MUST write such a control to 0.7 as one control with one way per kind of message, in the order CC, NRPN, RPN, whatever the order of the fields in the text. This order is a fixed convention of the converter; it is the order in which a tool that sends to the device prefers the ways (5.3.3).

A converter MUST write the CC as `ctrlType: cc` with `ctrlIndex: [cc]`, or `[cc, cc_lsb]` with `value_bits: 14`, and an NRPN or an RPN as `ctrlType: nrpn` or `rpn` with `ctrlIndex: [msb, lsb]` and `value_bits: 14`, the one coding of 0.4 and 0.5. A number 0 is a number of the source and is kept. A message of the mix that is not fully known (an NRPN or an RPN given by one number, a `cc_lsb` without its `cc`) has no way: it goes into the note `_incomplete` as 5.3.3 says, with the `range` of the control when the control keeps a way and without the `value_bits` the text does not give.

A converter MUST write the `range` of the control on every way and its `nrpn_lsb_range` on the NRPN way alone. It writes no `transmit`, `recognize` or `scale` and no note key for the mix, but for `_incomplete` and `_nrpn_lsb_range` (5.3.3); the other fields of the control convert as those of any control.

A converter MUST refuse, as `NotConvertible` at the control, `/cc_params/entries/N`, and return no text for, a control that mixes kinds of message and gives an `nrpn_lsb_range` without an NRPN, neither `nrpn_msb` nor `nrpn_lsb`: no way holds it.

A converter MUST refuse, as `NotConvertible`, a conversion in which a message of a control that mixes kinds and another control claim the same message on the same channel, a CC of 14 bits claiming its CC number and its LSB number: at the later control, `/cc_params/entries/N`, the rule naming the earlier one, and it returns no text.

A converter that converts to 0.6 MUST refuse a control of `cc_params` whose flat fields mix a CC, an NRPN or an RPN, as `NotConvertible` at the control, `/cc_params/entries/N`: 0.6 does not change.

A converter MUST refuse a control of a fingerprint (`controls`, `names[].control`) that mixes kinds of message, toward 0.7 as toward 0.6, as `NotConvertible` at the control, `/controls/N` or `/names/N/control`: it is reached by no way.

Cases: `0.7/convert/mixed-cc-and-nrpn-control-of-0-4-becomes-two-ways`, `0.7/convert/mixed-cc-and-nrpn-control-of-0-5-becomes-two-ways`, `0.7/convert/mixed-cc-and-rpn-control-becomes-two-ways`, `0.7/convert/mixed-nrpn-and-rpn-control-becomes-two-ways`, `0.7/convert/mixed-three-kinds-control-becomes-three-ways`, `0.7/convert/mixed-control-with-nrpn-of-one-number-kept-as-note`, `0.7/convert/mixed-control-with-cc-lsb-without-cc-kept-as-note`, `0.7/convert/mixed-control-with-block-without-nrpn-refused`, `0.7/convert/mixed-control-message-claimed-twice-refused`, `0.7/convert/fingerprint-control-mixing-cc-and-nrpn-refused`, `0.7/convert/fingerprint-name-control-mixing-cc-and-nrpn-refused`; the refusal toward 0.6 is `0.6/control-mixing-cc-and-nrpn-refused`.

### 21.4 A fingerprint always names what it was taken on

This section describes the fingerprints, at the level of the musician.

A fingerprint holds exactly one of two fields. `device` is a device with its `firmware` (required): either a catalogue device (`descriptor`, which names maker and model) or one the person declares (`vendor` and `model`). `program` is a firmware or application that runs alike on several devices, identified as its card writes it: `name`, `version`, `kind` (the words of a firmware's `kind`, `firmware` when absent). A capture taken on a device nobody has named yet is not a fingerprint: it stays in the tool that took it, and becomes one when the person declares maker, model and firmware.

A descriptor MUST give a fingerprint exactly one of `device` and `program`.

A reader MUST refuse, at the address of the fault:

- a fingerprint with neither `device` nor `program`, as `Missing` at `/device`;
- a fingerprint with both, as `BadValue` at `/program`;
- a `device` without `firmware`, at `/device/firmware`.

A converter MUST refuse, as `Missing`, a 0.5 fingerprint that has no device, at `/device`, or whose device holds no firmware, at `/device/firmware`.

> NOTE A signature that names nothing is of no use to whoever receives it, as an nmap signature belongs to a named class and a MIDI 2.0 Property Exchange DeviceInfo always carries manufacturer, model and version.

## 22 Controls at the precision of MIDI 2.0

### 22.1 The properties

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

A control entry of 0.6 (CC, 14-bit CC, NRPN, RPN) says what MIDI-CI Property Exchange Controller Resources (M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12) says of a control, so a tool lays controls out and an agent moves them without guessing. Each property of the table has its OSD field, or the reason it is not carried.

| Property | OSD field | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | `title` | The `title` of the control carries it; a control without one is written with a title derived from its role, its section or its address. |
| `description` | `description` | The `description` of the control carries it. |
| `channel` | `channel` | The norm: "channel — number (integer) (1-256, required)", counted from the first channel of the first Group of the Function Block and on across its Groups (3 Groups: Channels 1-48). OSD's `channel` is 0 to 15, one Group, with no Group of its own; the rule when none is given is in 22.5. |
| `ctrlType` | not carried | The kind of message is given by which address fields the entry holds (`cc`, `nrpn_msb`, `rpn_msb`, `p`). |
| `ctrlIndex` | not carried | The index is the number in those address fields (`cc`, `nrpn_lsb`…). |
| `priority` | `priority` | Same meaning as in M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12 (`priority`): 1 essential to 5 hidden. |
| `default` | `default` | The norm: "default — integer — The default value if entry is reset. Values declared in unsigned 32 bit values". OSD's `default` is the raw value in the space of `range`, in the scale of M2-115-U v1.0.2 (the controller resources, M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12, §1.5, give the values the scaling of the messages they describe): Min-Center-Max (§3) for a CC, an NRPN and an RPN of LSB 32 to 127, Zero-Extension (§4) for an RPN of LSB 0 to 31. A negative `default` is not written. |
| `transmit` | `transmit` | Same four words as M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12 (`transmit`): absolute, relative, both, none. |
| `recognize` | `recognize` | Same four words as M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12 (`recognize`): absolute, relative, both, none. |
| `numSigBits` | `value_bits` | The norm: "Number of significant bits used for the 32 bit values. 32, if not provided." The width of an NRPN or RPN value, 7 or 14, the two the norm gives (see 22.2); for a CC the bits follow from `range` and from `cc_lsb` (14 bits), a separate count could contradict them. |
| `paramPath` | not carried | OSD says it as `role`, a path of parts and a setting, which the reader writes back as a `paramPath`. |
| `typeHint` | `expects` | `expects` keeps its own six words; the correspondence is in 22.7. |
| `ctrlMapId` | carried by `values` | The names are inline in the entry (label with its integer), not the id of a map shared between controls; each value lies inside `range`. |
| `stepCount` | `step_count` | The number of steps, 2 or more. |
| `minMax` | `range` | The norm: "minMax — array of 2 integers […] Values declared in unsigned 32 bit values", with `numSigBits` for the bits used. OSD's `range` is the lowest and highest raw value, in the scale of M2-115-U v1.0.2 as `default` is (Min-Center-Max or Zero-Extension); a negative bound is not written. |

> NOTE A tool that exports to MIDI 2.0 writes the norm's channel as OSD's + 1, and one that imports reads it back as − 1 and refuses a channel above 16, naming the Group it falls in. The export scales `default` and `range` up to 32 bits and the import back down, by M2-115-U v1.0.2. These rules belong to the converters of osd-formats, which state them.

### 22.2 The value of an NRPN or an RPN

This section describes the bytes of a message.

An NRPN or an RPN selects its parameter by two controllers (the parameter MSB and LSB) and carries its value in the data entry controllers (CC 6 for the data MSB, CC 38 for the data LSB). A way that gives an NRPN or an RPN says the width of its value in `value_bits`, in the words of the norm. It is a field of the way, in 0.6 of the flat fields of an entry and of each way of `also`, in 0.7 of each way of `ways`. In 0.7 `value_bits` is required where the message does not imply the width, and optional (the implied width only) where it does (5.3.3); the rules below are those of 0.6.

A descriptor MUST give `value_bits` in a way that gives an NRPN or an RPN.

- `7`: the value is 7 bits, in the data MSB alone; its range lies from 0 to 127.
- `14`: the value is 14 bits, over the data MSB then the data LSB, the MSB first; its range lies from 0 to 16383.

A reader MUST refuse, at the field:

- a width other than 7 or 14;
- a missing width, as `Missing`;
- a `value_bits` on a way that gives neither an NRPN nor an RPN.

A converter MUST write `value_bits` 14 for an NRPN or an RPN of 0.5, the one coding 0.5 knew.

### 22.3 What `relative` says

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

`relative` is a word of the norm, in `transmit` and `recognize` (the same four words as M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12). The norm: "transmit — enum "absolute", "relative", "both", "none" — Declares whether this entry supports absolute values, relative values using Relative Registered/Assignable Controllers, both, or none." So a relative value is carried by the MIDI 2.0 Relative Registered Controllers and Relative Assignable Controllers, and the norm, not the device, says how it is coded there.

A reader MUST keep `relative` as written.

A reader MUST NOT restate the coding of a relative value.

> NOTE OSD 0.6 says no more than the word.

### 22.4 The block of an NRPN: `nrpn_lsb_range`

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

`nrpn_lsb_range` is `[lowest, highest]`, the parameter LSB numbers (as `nrpn_lsb`) of a block of NRPN parameters that one entry stands for, beside the `nrpn_msb` it shares. It is a field of 0.5 that 0.6 keeps as it was. It is not the range of the value (that is `range`).

A reader MUST keep `nrpn_lsb_range` as written.

A reader MUST write `nrpn_lsb_range` back.

A reader MUST NOT treat `nrpn_lsb_range` as the range of the value.

A reader MUST claim, for an entry with `nrpn_lsb_range`, only the message of its `nrpn_lsb`, and not the whole block.

> NOTE The converters of osd-formats neither read nor write it: each reports it as lost, since no target format has a place for it (case `read/nrpn-lsb-range`).

### 22.5 The channel of a control

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

A control's channel is the `channel` of its entry; when the entry gives none, the `channel` of its table (`cc_params.channel`); when neither gives one, the control has no channel of its own: it is the one the device is set to receive on, which no descriptor says.

A reader MUST take the channel of a control from its entry, else from the `cc_params.channel` of its table.

A reader MUST claim the message of a control that has no channel with no channel.

A reader MUST treat as the same message two controls with the same numbers and no channel, and as two different messages a control with a channel and a control without one.

### 22.6 A control reached by several ways

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

A setting that a device accepts by several messages (a CC and an NRPN, say) is one control, with one address. In 0.7 the control gives them in `ways`, one way each, in the order of preference (5.3.3); the rest of this section is the form of 0.6. The flat fields of the entry (`cc`, `cc_lsb`, `nrpn_msb`, `nrpn_lsb`, `rpn_msb`, `rpn_lsb`, `value_bits`, `range`) are its first way, as in 0.5 but for the width of an NRPN or RPN value; the optional `also`, a list, gives the ways after it, each with one message kind (a CC, a 14-bit CC with its `cc_lsb`, an NRPN or an RPN with its `value_bits`) and its own `range`. Two messages are the same when they share kind, channel and numbers; a 14-bit CC claims its CC number and its LSB number, so it collides with a simple CC on either.

A reader MUST refuse a way that mixes two kinds or names none, at its pointer.

A reader MUST give the ways in the order written: the flat way first, then `also` in order.

A reader MUST NOT reorder the ways.

A reader MUST refuse the later claim of a message, across the ways as across the controls and inside one control as between two, and name both places.

A converter MUST keep each control of a 0.5 descriptor as it is.

A converter MUST NOT join two controls.

> NOTE A tool that sends to the device takes the first way it can write that the device receives: a descriptor that writes a 14-bit CC or an NRPN before a 7-bit CC serves a tool that writes both. A tool that listens to the device reads every way it sends as the same control.

### 22.7 `expects` and `typeHint`

This section describes the controls at the level of the musician.

| `expects` | `typeHint` | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `continuous` | `continuous` | Same meaning. |
| `switch` | `toggle` | A control that stays on or off. |
| `momentary` | `momentary` | Same meaning. |
| `discrete` | `valueSelect` | A choice among named `values`. |
| `trigger` | `momentary` | The standard has no trigger: the nearest is momentary. The loss is OSD to MIDI 2.0 only; coming back, `momentary` reads as `momentary`. |
| `clock` | no `typeHint` | The standard has no clock: the control carries no hint. The loss is OSD to MIDI 2.0 only. |

The standard's `relative` has no `expects` word: it is carried by `transmit` and `recognize` (`relative` or `both`).

## 23 Resolution

### 23.1 Resolving a driver

This section describes how a firmware speaks MIDI, at the level of the musician and of the bytes.

Resolving a driver gives the device as its firmware runs it: the MIDI it shares from another driver (`midi_of`), then its blocks completed from maker profiles. A resolver takes a catalogue id and a source of files by id: a driver `maker/name` is the file `<maker>/<name>.json`, the profile `maker` is `<maker>/_profile` and the profile `maker/family` is `<maker>/_profile.<family>`.

A resolving reader MUST return the device, the ids of the files it read (the driver first) and its capabilities, or a fault of the kinds of section 4, in the file where it arises, at the pointer of the faulty place.

The rules of this section hold for every version read; the cases that hold them are those of `conformance/0.4/resolve/`, `conformance/0.5/resolve/` and `conformance/0.6/resolve/`. Each rule names its cases as `<version>/<case>`.

### 23.2 The version of a file with `midi_of`

This section describes how a firmware speaks MIDI, at the level of the musician.

What a `midi_of` file adds and declares follows the version of its own text, read before any conversion (23.5, 23.6, 23.9, 23.10 and 23.14).

A descriptor of 0.6 with `midi_of` MAY add parameters (its `params` holds `entries` only), add scripted commands and declare `capabilities`.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a 0.5 file with `midi_of` that declares `capabilities`, at `/capabilities`, even over a 0.6 target.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a 0.5 file with `midi_of` that gives `get_frame` in `params`, at `/params/get_frame`, even over a 0.6 target.

A resolving reader MUST judge a 0.6 file over a 0.4 or 0.5 target by the rules of 0.6, the target being converted to 0.6 and then joined.

### 23.3 A driver that names no other file is read as it is written

A resolving reader MUST read as it is written a driver that names no other file.

Cases: `0.5/resolve-plain-driver`

### 23.4 A file with `midi_of` keeps its identity keys and takes every other key from its target

The identity keys are the keys of `device` that say which firmware the file is: `name`, `vendor`, `osc_prefix` and `kind` (and `revision` for a pair of 0.6, below). The target is a driver of the same maker and device.

From 0.7 the identity keys are four: `name`, `vendor`, `osc_prefix` and `kind`; `device.revision` is removed (section 5.3.2). A resolving reader MUST choose the rule from the declared versions of the file and of its target, before any conversion: when the target or the file is of 0.7 or later, the four keys; when both are of 0.6 or earlier, the five keys of 0.6, `revision` among them, as 0.6 says (a published version does not change). A 0.6 file with `revision` on a 0.7 target takes the four keys, and `revision` does not count.

Cases: `0.7/resolve/midi-of-0-6-file-revision-ignored`

A descriptor that is the target of a `midi_of` MAY itself name another driver by `midi_of`.

A resolving reader MUST take the identity keys of the result from the file with `midi_of`, and every other key from its target.

A resolving reader MUST leave absent from the result an identity key that the file does not write.

A resolving reader MUST keep the `_` keys of the file, which are notes for people, and its credit as its own.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-own-keys`, `0.5/midi-of-identity-kept`, `0.5/midi-of-own-keys`

### 23.5 A file with `midi_of` adds route lists to its target's

The lists are `commands` and `custom_commands`.

A resolving reader MUST put the routes of the file after the routes of its target.

A descriptor of 0.6 that adds a script (`custom_commands` with `script`) MUST declare `capabilities: ["lua"]` itself, even when its target already brings Lua.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a 0.6 file that adds a script without `lua` in its `capabilities`, or with `[]`, at `/capabilities`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-routes-appended`, `0.6/midi-of-custom-commands-with-lua`, `0.6/midi-of-script-without-lua-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-script-with-empty-capabilities-refused`

### 23.6 A file with `midi_of` adds entries to the route tables of its target

The tables are `params`, `cc_params` and `triggers`.

A resolving reader MUST add the `entries` of a table of the file after the target's.

A resolving reader MUST take as it is from the file a table that the target does not have.

A resolving reader MUST read the `params` of a 0.6 file with `midi_of` as `entries` only, its message shape (`get_frame`, `set_frame`, `value_type`) coming from the target.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a 0.6 `params` taken as it is that lacks one of the three keys, as `Missing` at `/params/<key>`, in the file.

A resolving reader MUST require the three keys in a file without `midi_of`.

A resolving reader MUST judge the `control` of a setting of a sound transfer of a 0.6 file against the merged tables, whether or not the merged document keeps `midi_of`.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `control` that names an entry the merged tables lack, as `BadValue` at the `control`, in the file that writes it.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-routes-appended`, `0.5/midi-of-table-absent-in-target`, `0.6/midi-of-params-entries-added`, `0.6/midi-of-params-without-target-frames-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-absent-control-refused`

### 23.7 At the same OSC address the route of the file wins

A resolving reader MUST drop the route of the target at the OSC address where the file writes a route.

A resolving reader MUST put the route of the file after the other routes of the target.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-own-route-wins`

### 23.8 A file with `midi_of` replaces the blocks `identity` and `programs` whole

These blocks are how this firmware is recognised and what its program changes recall.

A resolving reader MUST replace the block of the target by the block of the file, whole and not key by key.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-own-block-replaces`

### 23.9 A file with `midi_of` adds routes only and a key that is its target's is refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse, as `UnknownField` at that key, any key of a file with `midi_of` other than `$schema`, `device`, `midi_of`, a `_` key, a route list, a route table, the two replaceable blocks and the keys this rule admits below.

A descriptor of 0.6 or 0.7 with `midi_of` MAY hold `capabilities`.

A descriptor of 0.7 with `midi_of` MAY hold `sources` (23.13).

A file with `midi_of` inherits its target and adds to it or replaces in it. So a descriptor of 0.7 with `midi_of` MAY hold `role_vocabulary`, the state the roles of its own routes are written with: the state of its target, the one the chain of the target gives, or any state when the target names none.

A resolving reader MUST give the result of such a file the state it names, and the roles of its own routes.

A resolving reader MUST refuse `role_vocabulary` in a file older than 0.7 with `midi_of`, as `UnknownField` at `/role_vocabulary`; a state other than its target's is refused first (23.35).

A resolving reader MUST refuse `capabilities` in a 0.5 file with `midi_of`, at `/capabilities`.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-target-keys-refused`, `0.5/midi-of-target-keys-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-key-outside-the-list-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-0-5-file-on-0-6-target-refused`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-chain-of-0-6-files-under-a-0-7-file`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-own-roles-under-the-target-state`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-own-roles-over-a-target-without-state`

### 23.10 Where the target has the route table the file adds entries only

A resolving reader MUST refuse another key of a table that the target has, as `UnknownField` at that key.

A resolving reader MUST refuse `get_frame`, `set_frame` and `value_type` in a 0.6 file, which it takes from its target (`/params/<key>`).

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-table-extra-key`, `0.6/midi-of-params-target-key-refused`

### 23.11 A route of the file has an OSC address

A resolving reader MUST refuse an entry without `osc`, as `Missing` at its `osc`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-route-without-osc`

### 23.12 A file writes each OSC address once in its own routes

A resolving reader MUST refuse a second route at an address already written in the same list or table, as `BadValue` at its `osc`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-route-twice`

### 23.13 The credit of the target is kept beside the file's own

A resolving reader MUST keep the `sources` of the file as its own. A `midi_of` file of 0.7 MAY add `sources`: they replace the target's in the result and are not carried to `midi_of_sources`.

A resolving reader MUST credit in `midi_of_sources` the `sources` of the targets followed, then the `midi_of_sources` they carry, in that order and without removing a repeat, and MUST NOT write `_midi_of_sources` for a target of 0.7. A `midi_of` file of 0.7 that carries `_sources` is refused as `BadValue` at `/_sources`, in its own file.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-own-keys`, `0.5/midi-of-credit`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-0-7-file-adds-sources`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-0-7-file-with-underscore-sources-refused`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-chain-of-0-6-files-under-a-0-7-file`

### 23.14 The capabilities a driver declares are kept as declared

The capabilities of a driver were checked on its own text.

A resolving reader MUST keep the capabilities that a driver declares.

A resolving reader MUST NOT replace the capabilities that a driver declares by its target's or a profile's.

A resolving reader MUST report a capability that only the merge requires (a profile or a target that brings Lua).

A resolving reader MUST NOT demand a capability that only the merge requires.

A descriptor of 0.6 with `midi_of` MAY declare `capabilities`, judged on its own text like any file: the list is the one its fields require, so a file that adds a script declares `["lua"]` and one that adds none declares `[]` or nothing.

A resolving reader MUST refuse another list, as `BadValue` at `/capabilities`.

A resolving reader MUST give as the resolved `capabilities` what the merged device requires, so those of both files, and as `inherited` what only the target brings.

A 0.5 file with `midi_of` declares no `capabilities`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-capabilities-declared-kept`, `0.5/profile-brings-lua`, `0.6/midi-of-capabilities-declared-over-target-lua`, `0.6/midi-of-lua-without-script-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-0-6-file-on-0-5-target`

### 23.15 The links of `midi_of` are followed, the target first

A resolving reader MUST follow the target to its own `midi_of` before it joins the file to it, so that a chain is joined from its end toward the file that was asked for.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-depth-8`, `0.5/midi-of-chain`

### 23.16 A `midi_of` is a catalogue id of two parts

A `midi_of` is a string `<maker>/<file>` without `.json`, each part non-empty and not starting with a dot.

A resolving reader MUST refuse any other `midi_of`, as `BadValue` at `/midi_of`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-id-malformed`, `0.5/midi-of-not-string`

### 23.17 A `midi_of` names a driver of the maker of the file

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `midi_of` that names another maker's driver, as `BadValue` at `/midi_of`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-other-maker`

### 23.18 A `midi_of` names a driver of the same device

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `midi_of` whose target has a `device.name` that differs from the file's, when the file writes one, as `BadValue` at `/midi_of`.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-other-device`

### 23.19 A `midi_of` whose target the catalogue does not hold is refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `midi_of` whose target the catalogue does not hold, as `LinkMissing` at `/midi_of`.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-missing-refused`, `0.5/midi-of-target-missing`

### 23.20 At most 8 `midi_of` links are followed from one file

A resolving reader MUST refuse a ninth `midi_of` link, as `LinkTooDeep` at `/midi_of`, in the file that writes it.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-depth-8`, `0.4/midi-of-depth-9-refused`, `0.5/midi-of-too-deep`

### 23.21 Links that come back to a file already followed are refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse links that come back to a file already followed, as `LinkCycle` at `/midi_of`, in the file that writes the link back.

Cases: `0.4/midi-of-cycle-refused`, `0.5/midi-of-cycle`

### 23.22 Two versions are joined in the more recent one

A resolving reader MUST convert the older text forward to the version of the newer one before it joins the two, so that the document states the more recent version, each link being compared with its own target.

A resolving reader MUST keep the `$schema` of a 0.5 file as the result's.

A resolving reader MUST refuse the resolution, in the file where a conversion fails, with the fault of the conversion.

Cases: `0.5/midi-of-older-target-converted`, `0.5/midi-of-newer-target-converts-the-file`, `0.5/midi-of-chain-older-targets-converted`, `0.5/midi-of-older-target-credit-kept`, `0.5/midi-of-older-target-not-convertible-refused`, `0.5/midi-of-schema-not-0-5-refused`

From 0.7 the versions of the profiles join too, since the form of a block differs (`identity.ports`, 5.3.7). A resolving reader MUST join the `midi_of` links first, then read the profile chains of the blocks `identity` and `programs`. When the joined device or one of these profiles is of 0.7, a resolving reader MUST convert every older text to 0.7, the joined device and each profile, before it merges any profile, so that the result states the `$schema` of 0.7 for the device and the nearest keys win as before (23.24 to 23.26). A resolving reader MUST refuse the resolution, in the file where such a conversion fails, with the fault of the conversion. With no text of 0.7 among them, the blocks are merged as each is written. A declared `lua` that no field of the converted device requires any more goes with the field that required it.

Cases: `0.7/resolve/profile-0-6-port-names-under-a-0-7-device`, `0.7/resolve/profile-0-7-under-a-0-6-device`, `0.7/resolve/profile-0-6-extending-a-0-7-profile`, `0.7/resolve/identity-from-a-0-7-profile-programs-from-a-0-6-one`, `0.7/resolve/profile-0-7-drops-the-lua-only-a-removed-field-required`

### 23.23 The profiles of the blocks are completed after the `midi_of` links

A resolving reader MUST complete, once the links are followed, each of the blocks `identity` and `programs` that names a `profile` from it.

Cases: `0.4/profile-own-keys-win`, `0.5/profile-own-keys-win`

### 23.24 A block naming a profile is the profile's block completed with the block's own keys

The profile of a maker is the file `<maker>/_profile`, the profile of a family the file `<maker>/_profile.<family>`.

A resolving reader MUST put the block's own keys in place of the profile's.

A resolving reader MUST keep in the block the name of the profile it wrote.

Cases: `0.4/profile-own-keys-win`, `0.5/profile-own-keys-win`, `0.5/profile-family-file`

### 23.25 A key a block sets to null drops the profile's key

Only a key directly in `identity` or `programs` (the blocks of 23.23), other than `profile`, takes `null`.

A reader MUST accept `null` on a key directly in `identity` or `programs`, other than `profile`.

A resolving reader MUST drop the profile's key from the resolved description when a block sets it to `null`.

A resolving reader MUST accept a `null` on a key that the profile does not give, and leave the key absent.

A reader MUST refuse a `null` inside the value of such a key (`programs.dump.request`, `identity.device_id.min`), because 23.24 replaces the whole key and does not merge into it.

A reader of 0.4 and 0.5 MUST refuse `null` on every direct key other than `identity.firmware`, as `BadValue` at that key, by the version of the file that carries it.

A reader of 0.6 MUST accept `null` on every optional key of the two blocks.

> NOTE The 0.4 and 0.5 schemas admit `null` on `identity.firmware` only.

Cases: `0.4/programs-dump-null-refused`, `0.4/identity-request-null-refused`, `0.5/programs-dump-null-refused`, `0.5/identity-request-null-refused`, `0.4/profile-null-removes-key`, `0.5/profile-null-drops-key`, `0.6/profile-null-drops-key`

### 23.26 A profile may itself name a profile and the nearest keys win

A resolving reader MUST complete a family profile's own block from the profile it names, before the driver's block is completed from it.

Cases: `0.4/profile-of-a-family`, `0.5/profile-chain`

### 23.27 A profile is a profile of the maker of the file

A resolving reader MUST refuse the profile of another maker, as `BadValue` at `/<block>/profile`.

Cases: `0.5/profile-other-maker`

### 23.28 A profile is a string `<maker>` or `<maker>/<family>`

A resolving reader MUST refuse a profile that is no string, or an id of three parts, as `BadValue` at `/<block>/profile`.

Cases: `0.5/profile-id-malformed`, `0.5/profile-not-string`

### 23.29 A profile that the catalogue does not hold is refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse a profile that the catalogue does not hold, as `LinkMissing` at `/<block>/profile`.

Cases: `0.4/profile-missing-refused`, `0.5/profile-file-missing`

### 23.30 A profile without the block named is refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse a profile without the block named, as `LinkMissing` at `/<block>/profile`.

Cases: `0.4/profile-without-the-block-refused`, `0.5/profile-block-missing`

### 23.31 At most 8 profile links are followed from one block

A resolving reader MUST refuse a ninth profile link, as `LinkTooDeep` at `/<block>/profile`, in the file that writes it.

Cases: `0.4/profile-depth-8`, `0.4/profile-depth-9-refused`, `0.5/profile-too-deep`

### 23.32 Profiles that name each other in a circle are refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse profiles that name each other in a circle, as `LinkCycle` at `/<block>/profile`, in the file that writes the link back.

Cases: `0.4/profile-cycle-refused`, `0.5/profile-cycle`

### 23.33 Each file is read strictly before it is merged

A resolving reader MUST refuse a target or a profile with a field its version does not define, by its reading, in its own file.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a text that is not JSON, as `Syntax` at the root.

Cases: `0.5/target-file-read-refused`, `0.5/syntax-refused`

### 23.34 A driver that the catalogue does not hold is refused

A resolving reader MUST refuse a driver that the catalogue does not hold, as `LinkMissing` at the root of the file asked for.

Cases: `0.5/driver-missing`

### 23.35 A file with `midi_of` that names another state of the role vocabulary than its target's is refused

Each file's roles are read against the state it names, and states are never merged. The rule applies only when the target and the file each name a `role_vocabulary` and the two differ; a file that names none keeps its target's.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a file with `midi_of` that names another state of the role vocabulary than its target's, as `BadValue` at `/role_vocabulary`, in the file that writes it.

Cases: `0.6/midi-of-role-vocabulary-state-differs-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-role-vocabulary-kept-from-target`, `0.7/resolve/midi-of-role-vocabulary-state-differs-refused`

### 23.36 A `stands_for` that the catalogue does not hold is refused

The rule holds for the `stands_for` of the resolved device, written by the file asked for or inherited from its `midi_of` target (a `midi_of` file does not take a `stands_for` of its own: it is not an identity key).

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `stands_for` that the catalogue does not hold, as `BadValue` at `/device/stands_for`, always in the file asked for.

Cases: `0.6/stands-for-absent-from-catalogue-refused`, `0.6/midi-of-inherited-stands-for-absent-refused`

### 23.37 An assembly resolves to the stock MIDI, then each module's routes in the listed order

Resolving an assembly gives the device as that firmware speaks MIDI, by the rules of `midi_of` applied once per module, in the listed order.

A resolving reader MUST apply what the MIDI file of a module writes, its own routes and the other keys of 23.9, over the MIDI accumulated (the stock's, then the earlier modules'), never what its chain resolves to.

A resolving reader MUST resolve an empty list of modules to the stock MIDI as it is.

A resolving reader MUST give the resolved device the stock's `device` block, and no `midi_of`.

A module that writes `identity` or `programs` replaces the block accumulated, whole (23.8).

A resolving reader MUST read the files in this order: the assembly's, then the modules', then the stock's.

Cases: `0.6/assembly-stock-and-two-modules-in-order`, `0.6/assembly-no-modules`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-module-replaces-identity-and-programs`

### 23.38 A module whose MIDI is the stock's adds nothing and one that names another descriptor is refused

A module names, in its target for the device, its MIDI either by `midi.descriptor`, a device descriptor whose `midi_of` is the stock firmware's MIDI descriptor, or by `midi.same_as`.

A resolving reader MUST add nothing for a `same_as` that names the stock firmware's MIDI descriptor.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a `same_as` that names another descriptor, as `BadValue` at `/targets/N/midi/same_as`, N the index of the module's target for the device, in the module's file.

Cases: `0.6/assembly-module-same-as-adds-nothing`, `0.6/assembly-module-same-as-another-refused`

### 23.39 The guards of `midi_of` hold for each module, and two modules that touch one address are not refused

The target of the MIDI file of a module is the MIDI accumulated: the stock's, then the earlier modules'.

A resolving reader MUST apply the guards of `midi_of` to each module, and refuse each fault in the module's MIDI file: a key that 23.9 refuses, a descriptor that names another maker's or another device's, one that repeats an address in its own file, or a `role_vocabulary` of another state than the MIDI accumulated names (23.35).

A resolving reader MUST replace, for two modules on one OSC address, the route of the earlier module by the later one's.

A resolving reader MUST NOT refuse or report two modules on one OSC address.

Cases: `0.6/assembly-module-midi-guard-refused`, `0.6/assembly-two-modules-same-address-no-refusal`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-modules-name-one-role-state`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-modules-name-two-role-states-refused`

### 23.40 An assembly resolves for one device, through each firmware's target for it

The device is a parameter of the reader, the descriptor id of a device (`elektron/digitone`); the format of the assembly does not change.

A resolving reader MUST read the MIDI of the stock firmware and of each module from the target whose `device.descriptor` is that id, so that the same assembly resolves on each device its firmware is made for, a module with a target per device.

A resolving reader MUST resolve an assembly without a device when the stock firmware has one target, whose device is then the one resolved for.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a firmware that has no target for the device, or two, as `BadValue` at `/targets`, in that firmware's file.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a stock firmware of several targets when no device is given, as `BadValue` at `/targets`.

Cases: `0.6/assembly-one-module-on-digitone`, `0.6/assembly-one-module-on-digitone-keys`, `0.6/assembly-module-without-target-for-device-refused`

### 23.41 A program is a stock of an assembly, and each device resolves to its MIDI, named or reused by `same_as`

An assembly whose stock is of kind `application` and whose list of modules is empty resolves each device the program runs on to the program's MIDI.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a stock that is neither of kind `firmware` nor of kind `application`, as `BadValue` at `/kind`, in its file.

A resolving reader MUST take the MIDI of a stock from the `midi.descriptor` of its target for the device, or from the descriptor its `midi.same_as` names.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a stock whose target has neither, as `BadValue` at `/targets/N/midi/descriptor`.

A resolving reader MUST refuse a module that is not of kind `module`, as `BadValue` at `/kind`, in its file; a module of kind `application` is refused so.

Cases: `0.6/assembly-program-on-first-device`, `0.6/assembly-program-on-second-device`

### 23.42 A resolution of 0.7 keeps the credit of the profiles (0.7)

A result of 0.7 takes blocks from profiles, and their credit goes with them. A profile of an older version joined into it gives the sources its `_sources` converts to (23.22, 5.3). A result older than 0.7 takes no source from a profile.

A resolving reader MUST credit, in a result of 0.7, the sources of every profile of the chain of each block, `identity` first, then `programs`, the nearest profile first, each profile file once.

A resolving reader MUST append to the result's `sources`, after the file's own, the sources of the profiles of a block the file asked for writes, creating the list when there is none.

A resolving reader MUST append to `midi_of_sources`, after the credit of the targets, the sources of the profiles of a block taken from a `midi_of` target, and MUST NOT add them to `sources`.

A resolving reader MUST credit once, in `sources`, a profile used by a block of the file and by a block of a target.

A resolving reader MUST give each appended source as the profile writes it, with the key `profile` holding the profile id as the block that names it writes it.

A profile without sources adds nothing.

Cases: `0.7/resolve/profile-sources-appended-to-sources`, `0.7/resolve/profile-sources-of-an-inherited-block-in-midi-of-sources`, `0.7/resolve/profile-sources-of-own-and-inherited-blocks-once`, `0.7/resolve/profile-sources-of-a-0-6-profile-converted`

### 23.43 A source of a profile never refuses a resolution and is not credited twice (0.7)

A resolving reader MUST give an appended source without its `id`, every other key unchanged, when the `sources` or `midi_of_sources` of the joined device, or an earlier appended source of a profile, already hold that `id`, and MUST NOT refuse the resolution for it.

A resolving reader MUST NOT append a source of a profile when the `sources` or `midi_of_sources` of the joined device hold, before any source of a profile is appended, an entry equal to it in every key but `id`, `profile` included; an entry that differs in another key hides nothing.

A resolving reader MUST judge the `source` of a reading against the sources written in the file and its `midi_of` targets only (5.3.4): a reading never names a source of a profile that the resolution appends (23.42), while an entry marked `profile` that one of those files writes itself stays in the set.

In the resolution of an assembly, a resolving reader MUST apply each module over the stock MIDI and the modules before it without any source of a profile, and MUST append those sources after the last module, each profile file once: to `sources` for a profile first credited by the last MIDI file applied, for a block that file writes, else to `midi_of_sources`.

> NOTE So resolving a resolved device again, alone or as a `midi_of` target, and applying a module of an assembly over MIDI already credited, add no repeat.

Cases: `0.7/resolve/profile-source-id-taken-given-without-id`, `0.7/resolve/profile-source-already-credited-not-repeated`, `0.7/resolve/profile-source-reading-refused`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-module-over-credited-midi-no-repeat`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-module-source-id-of-a-profile-source-accepted`, `0.7/resolve/assembly-module-reading-names-a-profile-source-refused`

## Annex A What 0.6 changes (informative)

0.6 is published and frozen. Its schemas: [`device`](../schemas/0.6/device.schema.json), [`profile`](../schemas/0.6/profile.schema.json), [`sound`](../schemas/0.6/sound.schema.json), [`fingerprint`](../schemas/0.6/fingerprint.schema.json), [`firmware`](../schemas/0.6/firmware.schema.json) and [`assembly`](../schemas/0.6/assembly.schema.json) (new). Its examples are in [`examples/0.6`](../examples/0.6), one per kind of document. `osd::convert_to(text, kind, Version::V0_6)` (`osd convert --to 0.6`) carries a 0.4 or 0.5 descriptor to it; the converter never invents a value that 0.6 adds, and a 0.6 text is never carried back. For a 0.5 file, "becomes" below is what the converter writes; a 0.5 file that uses nothing listed changes by its `$schema` alone.

- **A fingerprint names its subject**: a 0.6 fingerprint says what it was taken on, so that nobody receives a signature that names nothing. It holds exactly one of `device` (a catalogue `descriptor`, or the `vendor` and `model` the person declares, each with its `firmware`) and `program` (a firmware or application: `name`, `version`, `kind`). One that names neither is refused (`Missing` at `/device`). A 0.5 fingerprint converts when its device holds its firmware; without a device, or without the firmware, the conversion is refused and the file stays a 0.5 one.
- **Address**: the `$id` and `$schema` of each file name `0.6`. A 0.5 file becomes the same values under the 0.6 `$schema`.
- **`capabilities`** forbids a repeated entry (`uniqueItems`): the 0.6 reader refuses it, the 0.5 reader reads it once. The converter writes it once.
- **Null keys**: on `identity` and `programs`, an optional key may be `null` to drop what the maker profile gives.
- **Roles**: a control entry may carry a `role`, a path of known parts and a setting (`role_vocabulary` names the state of the vocabulary), or an extension. A 0.5 file has no role; none is added.
- **Controls**: an entry gives its `default`, `priority`, `step_count`, `transmit`, `recognize` and, for a CC, named `values`; one control may reach a message by several ways (`also`), and two controls that claim the same message are refused. An NRPN or RPN says its width (`value_bits`); a 0.5 one becomes 14 bits. A 0.5 file gains none of the others.
- **Banks and dumps**: a bank says `writable`; a dump says `reply_timeout_ms`.
- **Resources**: a device declares `resources` (the Deluge file write, by `steps`); absent in a 0.5 file.
- **Software devices**: `stands_for` and `fidelity` say which instrument a software device imitates.
- **Sounds**: a sound has `external_ids`, `needs` (the modules it needs, each with an optional minimum version) and `made_on` (the assembly it was made on); a sound has no `modules` field. Sound transfers (`sound_transfers`) and their `places` (not `settings`, the word of `sound.content.settings`) read a sound setting by setting, and give it an identity. The coding of the data in 7 bits is `coding` (`none` or `msb_first_7in8`), the word of the `write` step of a file write too (`mcoded7`, the Mcoded7 of M2-103-UM 6.1.7, or `msb_first_7in8`); `identity.unpack` of 0.5 keeps its spelling. A 0.5 sound gains none of them.
- **One spelling**: a hex text is upper-case hexadecimal; the content of a sound holds at least one of `sysex`, `midi`, `settings`; the flat fields of a control never mix a CC, an NRPN and an RPN; a control of a fingerprint knows `cc`, `cc_lsb`, `nrpn_msb`, `nrpn_lsb`, `rpn_msb`, `rpn_lsb`, one kind each. From a 0.5 file the converter writes a lower-case hex text in upper case, the same bytes; in the conversion to 0.6 it refuses (`NotConvertible`) a control that mixes kinds, at the control (the conversion to 0.7 writes a control of `cc_params` as ways, 21.3), and a sound without content, at the content: it chooses and invents nothing.
- **Open lists**: the name of a resource is any name (Property Exchange's `X-` is not required) and `export` any non-empty name of a format.
- **Sound `kind`**: besides the nine words, an extension `x-<registrant>-<term>` (the `selects` form). The reader keeps the value and treats it as a `sound` (a `device` is required, only `clip` is without one). 0.4 and 0.5 stay closed.
- **Firmware**: it names `targets`, each with its device, its own `midi`, what it `runs_on` and its lineage; the first-level `device`, `midi` and `based_on` are refused. A 0.5 firmware becomes one target. The kinds `module` and `application` (a program installed on a host system) and a `builder` exist.
- **Host device without MIDI**: a device with a USB identity and no MIDI of its own is valid, a host for programs.
- **Versions** that are compared (base versions, minimum versions) follow one grammar: letters, digits and dots, with a digit; outside it, a comparison answers unknown.
- **Assembly**: a new document, the stock firmware and the modules applied, in order, each with its exact version. A 0.5 file has none.
- **`midi_of`**: a file that follows a target adds only `params` entries; it takes the frames from its target and declares `lua` itself when it adds a script.
- **Fingerprint**: its controls take the words of a device's control (see *One spelling*); apart from them, unchanged from 0.5.

**Taken out of 0.6.** Nothing that 0.5 published: the fifteen fields of 0.5 that had no case (`maker_replies`, `identity_reply`, `names`, `sends`, `unpack`, `orientation`, `loads_sounds_of`, `channel_mode`, `first_pc`, `first_number`, `note_offset`, `rate_limit_hz`, `wrap_sysex`, `license_terms`, `derived_from`) stay, each defined in this text and shown by a case. What went out of the 0.6 drafts: the closed list of `export` names and the obligatory `X-` of a resource name (the format names no tool), and the words `packing` and `settings` of a sound transfer (renamed `coding` and `places`).

**Left for a later version.** What is absent from 0.6, not false: MPE, MIDI 2 Groups, multitimbral parts, per-note controllers, several get/set frames per device, two-byte checksums, dumps of variable length or in several messages.
