# The vocabulary of roles

## 1 Scope

A control can say what it does in words every device shares, so that a person or an agent asks "shorten the attack of the filter's envelope" without knowing the device, and what is written for one device works on another. That is the control's **role**. The roles are a vocabulary written in OSD as data, under `schemas/vocabulary/<state>/`, and a role is as precise as MIDI 2.0 lets a device say it: the names are those of the standard, and the form is the path of its Property Exchange.

This text describes the roles at the level of the musician. It specifies the vocabulary of roles that [the text of OSD 0.6](../0.6.md) refers to by `role_vocabulary`.

## 2 Conformance

The classes are a descriptor, a reader, a resolving reader and a converter, as [the text of OSD 0.6](../0.6.md) defines them in its sections 2 and 4, and a publisher, the class of [ERRATA.md](../ERRATA.md): the one that enters a term into a state of the vocabulary. The key words are those of section 2.2 of the text of OSD 0.6, with the meaning of RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

## 3 What a role is

A role is a **path**: one part or more, each with its number when the instrument has several, then one setting. It is a JSON object on a control (a CC, NRPN or RPN entry, a `params` entry), never a string to cut.

- `parts`: the path, from the outermost part to the innermost, at least one. Each is `{part, index?}`: a term of type `part`, and its number, from 1, when the instrument has several (`oscillator` 2).
- `setting`: what is set on the last part (`cutoff`, `attack-time`, `pan`…), a term of type `setting`.

A descriptor MUST write a role as a JSON object.

A descriptor MUST NOT write a role as a string to cut.

A reader MUST refuse the flat form of a role (one part, an index, a setting).

A reader MUST accept a control without a role.

A converter MUST NOT add a role to a 0.5 descriptor that it converts to 0.6.

A descriptor that carries a role MUST name, once and in the top-level field `role_vocabulary` (`"1"`), the state of the vocabulary every role was written with.

A reader MUST refuse a descriptor with a role and no `role_vocabulary`.

A reader MUST refuse a `role_vocabulary` that names a state the reader does not hold.

A maker profile carries no control, so no `role_vocabulary`. A driver and the profile or the `midi_of` target it links are each read against their own file.

A resolving reader MUST read the roles of each file against the state that file names.

A resolving reader MUST NOT rewrite a role.

A resolving reader MUST NOT merge states.

> EXAMPLE
>
> ```json
> { "osc": "/filter/cutoff", "cc": 74, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }], "setting": "cutoff" } }
> { "osc": "/fenv/attack", "cc": 20, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "attack-time" } }
> { "osc": "/aenv/attack", "cc": 16, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "amplifier" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "attack-time" } }
> { "osc": "/osc2/pitch", "cc": 36, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "oscillator", "index": 2 }], "setting": "pitch" } }
> ```

> NOTE The attack of the filter's envelope and the attack of the amplifier's are two roles, which the flat form of earlier drafts (one part, an index, a setting) could not tell apart. A `midi_of` file that names another state than its target's is refused at `/role_vocabulary` (section 23.35 of the text of OSD 0.6).

## 4 The role as a `paramPath`

MIDI-CI Property Exchange (Controller Resources, M2-117-UM v1.0, tables 6 and 12) gives each control a `paramPath`, a path of parts ending in what is set; its example is `/Oscillator/A/Envelope/Attack`. OSD roles are the same path, kept as structure in the JSON.

| Role | `paramPath` |
|---|---|
| parts `filter`, setting `cutoff` | `/Filter/Cutoff` |
| parts `filter`, `envelope`, setting `attack-time` | `/Filter/Envelope/AttackTime` |
| parts `amplifier`, `envelope`, setting `attack-time` | `/Amplifier/Envelope/AttackTime` |
| parts `oscillator` 2, setting `pitch` | `/Oscillator/2/Pitch` |
| parts `oscillator` 2, `envelope` 1, setting `attack-time` | `/Oscillator/2/Envelope/1/AttackTime` |
| parts `filter`, `x-acme-ladder`, setting `cutoff` | `/Filter/x-acme-ladder/Cutoff` |

A reader MUST give the `paramPath` of a role by these rules:

- each term is its CamelCase segment: the words between hyphens start with a capital letter and the hyphens go (`attack-time` is `AttackTime`);
- the number of a part is a numeric segment after it;
- the setting is the last segment;
- an extension `x-<registrant>-<term>` is written as it is, its case unchanged.

A reader MUST read a `paramPath` text back to the same role against a named state: a CamelCase segment is a term of the state, a segment of the form of an extension is an extension, and a number is the index of the part before it.

A reader MUST refuse any other segment of a `paramPath`, at its place.

> NOTE Two terms of a state that say the same segment (`x1` and `x-1`, both `X1`) make the state refused (section 9), so the text always reads back to one role.

## 5 What a reader checks

A reader MUST check each role against the state the descriptor names.

A reader MUST refuse, at the faulty field, a `part` that is not a term of type `part`, and a `setting` that is not a term of type `setting`.

A reader MUST refuse, at that part, a part that does not stand where its term says: the first part opens a path (`at_top`), and each next part stands under the one before it (`stands_under`). The index is carried by each part and does not change where a part stands.

A reader MUST refuse, at the role, a setting that does not apply to the last part (`applies_to` of the setting names it): `filter` with `pan`, or `filter` and `envelope` with `cutoff`.

A reader MUST read a role of known terms as a known role, valid without being listed.

A reader MUST read a role that holds an extension, even in the middle of a path, as no known role.

A reader MUST NOT check what an extension of a role touches.

A term that is neither known nor an extension is refused at the term.

A descriptor MAY write a term that the state lacks as an extension `x-<registrant>-<term>`, in the form and the registry of 0.5 (`^x-[a-z0-9]+-[a-z0-9-]+$`, `schemas/extensions.md`), `<registrant>` being the name the author chooses.

> NOTE Nothing in a reader is written per role: a reader resolves a descriptor's roles against the state's data.

## 6 Ten roles of a real device (informative)

The Korg minilogue has a filter envelope (its EG, aimed at the cutoff) and an amplifier envelope; `examples/0.6/minilogue.device.json` gives its controls with their roles. Ten of them:

```json
{ "osc": "/amp/env/attack", "cc": 16, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "amplifier" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "attack-time" } }
{ "osc": "/amp/env/release", "cc": 19, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "amplifier" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "release-time" } }
{ "osc": "/eg/attack", "cc": 20, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "attack-time" } }
{ "osc": "/eg/decay", "cc": 21, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }, { "part": "envelope" }], "setting": "decay-time" } }
{ "osc": "/lfo/rate", "cc": 24, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "lfo" }], "setting": "rate" } }
{ "osc": "/delay/time", "cc": 31, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "delay" }], "setting": "delay-time" } }
{ "osc": "/vco1/pitch", "cc": 34, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "oscillator", "index": 1 }], "setting": "pitch" } }
{ "osc": "/vco2/pitch", "cc": 36, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "oscillator", "index": 2 }], "setting": "pitch" } }
{ "osc": "/filter/cutoff", "cc": 43, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }], "setting": "cutoff" } }
{ "osc": "/filter/resonance", "cc": 44, "role": { "parts": [{ "part": "filter" }], "setting": "resonance" } }
```

Their `paramPath`s are `/Amplifier/Envelope/AttackTime`, `/Amplifier/Envelope/ReleaseTime`, `/Filter/Envelope/AttackTime`, `/Filter/Envelope/DecayTime`, `/Lfo/Rate`, `/Delay/DelayTime`, `/Oscillator/1/Pitch`, `/Oscillator/2/Pitch`, `/Filter/Cutoff` and `/Filter/Resonance`.

## 7 A term

Each term of a state has:

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `name` | its one name |
| `type` | `part` or `setting` |
| `definition` | its definition, in one sentence |
| `applies_to` | for a setting, the parts (terms of type `part` of the same state) it applies to; only the last part of a path is held against it |
| `stands_under` | for a part, the parts it can come under in a path (`envelope` under `filter`, `amplifier`, `oscillator`, `voice`) |
| `at_top` | for a part, whether it can open a path; true when `stands_under` is absent, false when it is present, unless the part writes it (`lfo` stands under `oscillator`, `filter` and `amplifier` and opens a path too) |
| `words` | the makers' words that mean it, each `{word, origin}`: the origin is the specification (the MIDI 1.0 table, M2-104-UM, Property Exchange) or the maker that uses the word |
| `source` | where it comes from: `{controller, name}`, the entries of the MIDI 1.0 table of defined controllers whose composition uses the term; and `{document, table, number?, name}`, a row of a published table (`M2-104-UM v1.1.2`, table `22`, row `73`, `Sound Controller 4 (Attack Time)`; `M2-117-UM v1.0`, tables `6` and `12`). The documents are cited, never copied |
| `shared_by` | where a term the standard does not name is shared: a list of `{maker, device}`, two devices of two makers at least |
| `replaced_by` | the term of the same state that replaces it, when a later decision replaces it |

A publisher MUST cite in the `source` of a term the standard it is taken from.

A publisher MUST NOT add a term that the standard leaves to each maker (`oscillator`, `mixer`, `arpeggiator`…) unless two devices of two different makers have it.

> NOTE A function is **composed**, not listed once per case. MIDI 1.0 "Brightness" (controller 74) is `filter` + `cutoff`: each cites controller 74 in its `source`, and "Brightness" is a maker's word of `cutoff`, not a term. Likewise controller 5 "Portamento Time" is `voice` + `portamento-time`, and controller 10 "Pan" is `output` + `pan`.

## 8 What State 1 takes from MIDI 2.0 (informative)

The names of M2-104-UM v1.1.2, table 22 (Registered Per-Note Controllers) that set a sound are held, each as a term or a maker's word of a term, with its row in `source`:

| Row | Name | Held as |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 11 | Modulation, Breath, Expression | settings `modulation`, `breath`, `expression` of the part `performance`, what a player moves |
| 3 | Pitch 7.25 | the word of `pitch`, on `voice` |
| 7, 8, 10 | Volume, Balance, Pan | `level` (Volume is its word), `balance` and `pan`, on `output` |
| 70 | Sound Controller 1 (Sound Variation) | `variation` on `sound` |
| 71, 74 | Timbre/Harmonic Intensity, Brightness | `resonance` and `cutoff` on `filter` |
| 72, 73, 75 | Release, Attack, Decay Time | `release-time`, `attack-time`, `decay-time` on `envelope` |
| 76, 77, 78 | Vibrato Rate, Depth, Delay | `rate`, `depth`, `delay-time` on the part `vibrato` |
| 91 to 95 | Effects 1 to 5 Depth | `depth` on `reverb`, `effect` (Tremolo, Celeste, Phaser are its words), `chorus`; Reverb Send Level and Chorus Send Level are words of `depth` |

Row 79, Sound Controller 10, is undefined: it has no term. The standard names no oscillator, LFO, mixer or sequencer, which it leaves to each maker: the parts `oscillator`, `noise`, `mixer`, `filter`, `amplifier`, `envelope`, `lfo`, `effect` (with `delay`, `reverb`, `chorus` and `equalizer`, kinds of effect that open a path), `arpeggiator`, `sequencer`, `voice`, `output` and the settings they carry are state 1's, each with the devices that share it.

## 9 The rule of entry

A publisher MUST enter a role into the vocabulary only when it has a specific function that the existing roles cannot name, alone or put together.

A publisher MUST NOT let the same function in twice.

A reader MUST refuse a state that holds:

- a name present twice, a maker's word present under two terms (in any case), or a word that is the name of another term, at the second one;
- two terms whose CamelCase segments are the same, at the second name;
- a term without a definition of one sentence, a type, a `source` or a `shared_by`, or a word without the origin of each word; a `shared_by` of fewer than two devices or of one maker, at the term;
- a term whose name or word is a path of parts and a setting the state already holds (`brightness` next to `filter` and `cutoff`, `filter-envelope-attack-time` next to `filter`, `envelope` and `attack-time`), or a setting whose controllers a part and another setting already compose, at the term;
- an `applies_to`, a `stands_under` or a `replaced_by` that names no term, or one that names a term that is not a part;
- a setting that writes `stands_under` or `at_top`.

> NOTE A term enters a state of the vocabulary as a proposal, which follows the states and the delay written in [PROCESS.md](../PROCESS.md).

> NOTE `osd` and the schema of the vocabulary hold what a machine can hold. The schema of the vocabulary states the shapes (a source by controller or by document, an origin, a definition of one sentence, `applies_to` for a setting, `stands_under` and `at_top` for a part, `shared_by`); comparing two terms is `osd`'s, since a JSON Schema cannot.
